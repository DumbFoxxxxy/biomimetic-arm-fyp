Kinematically-Constrained Redundant
Cable-Driven Parallel Robots: Modeling,
Redundancy Analysis, and
Stiffness Optimization
Hamed Jamshidifar, Amir Khajepour, Baris Fidan , Senior Member, IEEE, and Mitchell Rushton
Abstract—This paper develops a general model for
kinematically-constrained redundant cable-driven parallel
robots (CDPRs), and studies stiffness improving effects
and redundancy resolution of such robots aiming stiffness
optimization to minimize their undesired perturbations un-
der external disturbances in a desired direction. In the de-
veloped model, assuming an axially ﬂexible model for the
cables, motion equation is derived. Considering the role
of constrained cables in restriction of CDPR’s rotational
degrees of freedom, the vibration equation of the moving
platform is separated from the equation of motion. The re-
sulted vibration equation is a linear dynamic system with a
stiffness matrix formed by the cables’ tension and the con-
strained cables’ axial stiffness. Based on that, the substan-
tial effects of constrained cables and the potential effects of
cables’ tension on the stiffness improvement of CDPRs are
shown. Accordingly, the cables’ tension redundancy prob-
lem is formulated. Redundancy resolution is studied con-
sidering the directional stiffness of the moving platform as
the objective function to maximize. This objective function
is derived as a linear function of cables’ redundant tensions
and the corresponding redundancy problem solved by us-
ing a time-efﬁcient method of linear programming. The de-
veloped model and the proposed redundancy resolution ap-
proach are experimentally tested on an actual warehousing
robot to maximize its translational stiffness. Comparison
of theoretical and experimental results demonstrates the
validity of the proposed optimization approach and the ef-
fectiveness of kinematically-constrained actuation method.
Index Terms—Flexible structures, mechanical cables, op-
timization methods, redundancy, robots.

a desired workspace. Large workspaces, low moving inertia,
high accelerations, and fast motions are the most signiﬁcant
advantages of the CDPRs. Nevertheless, CDPRs suffer from
low stiffness and the need for keeping tension in the cables.
Among different types of CDPRs, redundant CDPRs are dis-
tinguished by having more actuators than the moving platform’s
degrees of freedom (DOFs). Redundancy of actuators not only
provides a larger workspace for the CDPRs, but also can im-
prove their stiffness.
In addition to the actuation redundancy, a CDPR can beneﬁt
from kinematic constraints in its actuation. In kinematically-
constrained actuation method, one or more actuators, named
constrained actuators, are connected to several cables and held
under position control to provide the nonstretched length of
their cables. Simultaneously, the rest of cables provide big
enough tensions to make the tension of constrained cables.
Compared to the common type of actuation, kinematically-
constrained CDPRs beneﬁt from a signiﬁcantly higher level of
stiffness.
In the majority of CDPRs, ranging from the fast load con-
veyance (FALCON) [1] to the recent CDPRs [2], [3] there is
no kinematic constraint in the cables’ actuation. In recent years,
kinematically-constrained CDPRs have attracted more atten-
tion. A design approach is provided by Behzadipour and Khaje-
pour for a kinematically-constrained CDPR to restrict the rota-
tional DOFs of a ﬂat moving platform [4]. Bosscher et al. have
proposed the conceptual design of a suspended kinematically-
constrained CDPR as a search and rescue robotic system [5]. In
their CDPR design, the rotational DOFs of a moving platform
are constrained to provide a pure translational motion over a
three-dimensional (3-D) workspace. Torres-Mendez and Kha-
jepour have introduced a kinematically-constrained CDPR for
warehousing applications [6]. Adaptive control problem of a
simpliﬁed version of the introduced CDPR is studied in [7].
Saber has demonstrated the effects of kinematic constraints on
improvement of CDPRs’ workspace [8]. A similar actuation
technique, named cable differentials, is proposed by Khakpour
et al. to improve the workspace and kinetostatic properties of
CDPRs [9]. In this method, by using multiple idle pulleys,

In addition to design, geometry optimization and workspace
analysis of CDPRs, the problem of CDPRs’ actuation redun-
dancy has been studied by a number of researchers. In recent
decades, various optimization objective functions and resolution
techniques have been proposed and studied. According to the
desired characteristic of the CDPRs, different objective func-
tions of cables’ tensions such as 1-norm, /221e-norm, and p-norm
(1 < p < /221e) [10], [11], have been proposed. Shiang et al.
have considered the summation of cables’ tensions as the op-
timization objective function, and they have proposed use of
linear programming (LP) for redundancy resolution [12]. Pott
et al. have focused on Euclidian norm of cables’ tensions as
the optimization objective function and have studied different
methods for its minimization [13]. A few studies have focused
on stiffness related objective functions. Y u et al. have studied
the idea of holding a predetermined stiffness for the moving
platform [14]. In another study, the same group has developed
an algorithm to control a lower bound of the robot stiffness via
holding the smallest eigenvalue of the stiffness matrix bigger
than a predeﬁned value [15].
Beside the proposed optimization objective functions, ﬁnd-
ing a time efﬁcient redundancy resolution technique has re-
ceived a great deal of interest by different researchers. LP [16],
quadratic programming [17], Dykstra’s projection technique as
an iterative approach [18], an analytic-iterative method based on
Karush–Kuhn–Tucker theorem [19], and improved closed-form
technique [20] are among the proposed redundancy resolution
approaches.
In spite of the numerous studies on different CDPRs based on
the best knowledge of the authors, the effect of kinematically-
constrained actuation on stiffness improvement and disturbance
rejection of the CDPRs within their motion is not studied so
far. Previous studies, including [21], [22], have considered both
the axial stiffness and tension of all cables in formulation of
CDPR’s stiffness matrix. Such consideration is valid only for
the static conditions, where all cables can be considered ﬁxed
at their connection points with the ground.
In addition, a small number of studies have focused on stiff-
ness related objective functions; whereas the CDPRs suffer from
their low stiffness in different applications. To the best of our
knowledge no one has considered the magnitude of directional
stiffness of moving platform as an objective function. Maximiz-
ing the moving platform stiffness in a desired direction provides
the maximum resistance to external disturbances in that direc-
tion. This approach can be utilized to maximize the moving
platform stiffness in its softest direction to minimize the moving
platform perturbations under external disturbances. In addition,
this approach can be utilized for stiffness maximization in the
most interactive direction.
Accordingly, by the goal of demonstrating the effects of
kinematically-constrained actuation on stiffness improvement
and also maximizing the moving platform rotational or
translational stiffness in a desired direction, both problems of
vibration modeling and actuation redundancy of kinematically-
constrained redundant CDPRs are addressed in this paper.
Fig. 1. Layout of a redundant planar CDPR with kinematic constraints.
For the purpose of redundancy resolution, the magnitude of
directional stiffness of moving platform is formulated as a
linear objective function of cable tensions and is maximized
by using LP , allowing real-time implementation. The proposed
actuation method and stiffness optimization approach are
experimentally evaluated on an actual planar warehousing
CDPR.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section II pro-
vides a general ﬂexible model for kinematically-constrained
CDPRs and formulates the actuation redundancy problem.
Section III formulates the cables’ tension redundancy problem
and Section IV develops the redundancy resolution approach
to maximize the directional stiffness of the moving platform.
In Section V, the proposed approach is used for modeling and
stiffness maximization of a planar CDPR. Section VI presents
the experimental tests for validating the proposed approach.
Concluding remarks are provided in Section VII.
II. K INEMA TICALL Y-CONSTRAINED CABLE-DRIVEN ROBOTS
In this section, with the help of an illustrative example, we
review the kinematic constraints in actuation of CDPRs. Fur-
thermore, we derive the motion and vibration equations of a
general kinematically-constrained redundant CDPR.
A. Kinematic Constraints in a CDPR
In order to move the platform of a common redundant CDPR,
such as the planar CDPR of Fig. 1 with four cables of 1–4, three
DOFs of x, y, θ, and a desired translational motion, each cable
force is controlled by an individual actuator to provide a desired
tension. In such conditions, the moving platform stiffness within
the motion is only formed by the cables’ tension.
By adding two cables of 5 and 6, driven by actuators of 1 and
2, we can add a kinematic constraint to the moving platform
to restrict the undesirable DOF of θ. To move the platform by
using 6 cables, consider top actuators under position control to
provide the nonstretched length of top cables. Simultaneously,
to make the desired tension of top cables, the desired tension
of bottom cables are provided by their actuators. In such
conditions, the platform stiffness within the motion is formed
by not only the cables’ tension, but also the axial stiffness of
constrained cables which can be considerably high to resist
θ. Accordingly, a general ﬂexible kinematically-constrained

B. General Model of Kinematically-Constrained CDPRs
In the structure of the general kinematically-constrained
CDPR, the nonconstrained cables are replaced with their ten-
sions and each constrained cable is considered as a massless
linear spring with small damping effects and ignorable slack-
ness. It is assumed that all cables of each constrained actuator
have an equal initial (nonstretched) length and also all actua-
tors are perfect in providing the initial length or tension of their
connected cables.
Figs. 2 and 3 show the general layout and model of a 3-D
kinematically-constrained redundant CDPR with n = n′ + n′′
actuators and m = m1 + ... + mn′ + n′′ cables, where n′ and
n′′ denote the number of constrained and non-constrained ac-
tuators. In this conﬁguration, initial and total lengths of cable j
driven by actuator i, are denoted by loi and lji . The unit and an-
chor vectors (with respect the moving platform center of mass)
for the same cable are designated by uji and rji . The tension
vector of each cable is along uji and has magnitude τji .T h e
number of constrained cables of the ith actuator is designated
by mi and the vectors of external force and moment applied on
the moving platform center of mass are denoted byFe and Me .
Assuming a known position and orientation for the moving
platform, the magnitudes of the cable tensions isτji = kji (lji −
loi ), in which kji = aE/lji denotes the stiffness of each cable
where E and a are Y oung’s modulus of elasticity and the cable
cross section area.
To review the geometrical properties of kinematically-
constrained actuation, consider the nominal conditions of the
moving platform where we have no perturbation in the position
or orientation of the moving platform. In such conditions, the
total length of all cables of each constrained actuator needs to be
equal. Accordingly, the connection points of constrained cables
need to be selected such that this condition holds all over the
workspace. In addition, the spool system of each constrained
actuator needs to provide equal lengths for its connected cables.
Applying large enough tensions, via nonconstrained cables,
holds all the constrained cables under tension to act as a strong
spring, which, by proper selection of the cables’ connection
points, can highly improve the moving platform rotational
stiffness.

IV . REDUNDANCY RESOLUTION
In this paper, it is aimed to ﬁnd the optimum α in polytope
(22), which maximizes the stiffness of the moving platform in
a desired direction. To ﬁnd the desired direction, two different
scenarios are considered. In the ﬁrst scenario, we consider that
the moving platform interaction with its environment or a tool
mounted on it, makes a disturbing force Fe or moment Me in
a known direction. In such a case, the corresponding objective
function is derived based on the translational or rotational part of
K in direction ofFe orMe and then is maximized. Accordingly,
we can expect to have the minimum translational or rotational
deviation under disturbingFe or Me in their applied directions.
In the second scenario, in absence of any knowledge about the
disturbing force or moment, we have ﬁrst considered the coupled
structural stiffness matrix Kk to deﬁne the softest direction of
the stiffness matrix. Next, the corresponding objective function
of softest direction is deﬁned and maximized.

B. Stiffness Maximization in the Softest Direction
To deﬁne the softest direction of a CDPR, we have assumed
that the moving platform stiffness matrix K is made only by the
structural part Kk . In such conditions, a disturbing wrench of
[Fe, Me ]T = δDβ β makes the perturbationδXββ, whereβ =
[βx βy βz βθx βθy βθz ]T is a unit vector. The softest direction
of the moving platform is deﬁned as the vector β for which
δXβ/δDβ has its maximum value. Noting Kk as a positive
deﬁnite symmetric matrix [23], it can be shown that β = eλmin
whereeλmin is the eigenvector ofKk minimum eigenvalueλmin .
Accordingly, the maximum value of δXβ/δDβ is obtained as
1/λmin . Noting the total stiffness matrix K = Kk + K¯τ ,i n
order to minimize δXβ/δDβ in β direction, maximization of
the objective function
Kβ = βT Kβ,β = eλ min (33)
is formulated as
K∗β = maxα Kβ (α). (34)
Utilizing the linearity of the obtained objective functions,
LP can be used as the redundancy resolution technique for the
developed cost functions. As an example, this algorithm for the

maximizeKβtrans (α)
over α ∈ Rn −η
subject to τ min − A′+w′ ≤ Hα ≤ τ max − A′+w′. (35)
Solving (35) gives α∗ as the optimized vector of α. Substi-

actuators’ input calculation. In addition, based on the optimized
τ ′, the stiffness matrix K can be obtained and used in vibration
(15). Accordingly, the free vibration of the moving platform in

VII. C ONCLUSION
A general model of the ﬂexible redundant kinematically-
constrained CDPRs has been developed and the effects of
cables’ kinematic constraints and redundant tensions on the
stiffness of CDPRs are studied. By the goal of perturbation
minimization, maximization of the stiffness of moving platform
in a desired direction has been proposed as a linear objective
function to address the redundancy problem of the actuators.
The proposed approach has been developed generally and used
in modeling and stiffness maximization of a planar redundant
warehousing robot as a case study. Experimental evaluation of
natural frequency of the moving platform for optimized and
nonoptimized tensions and also different payloads has demon-
strated validity of the proposed approach. Agreement between
the theoretical and experimental results conﬁrms acceptabil-
ity of our assumptions in negligibility of the impacts of ca-
bles’ damping effects as well as spools systems’ uneven tension
distribution on the natural frequency of the moving platform.
