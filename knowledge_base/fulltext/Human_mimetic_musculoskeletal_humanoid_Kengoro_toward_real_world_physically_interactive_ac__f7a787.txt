Human Mimetic Musculoskeletal Humanoid Kengoro toward
Real World Physically Interactive Actions
Y uki Asano, Toyotaka Kozuki, Soichi Ookubo, Masaya Kawamura, Shinsuke Nakashima,

Abstract— We have been developing human mimetic mus-
culoskeletal humanoids from the view point of human-inspired
design approach. Kengoro is our latest version of muscu-
loskeletal humanoid designed to achieve physically interactive
actions in real world. This study presents the design concept,
body characteristics, and motion achievements of Kengoro. In
the design process of Kengoro, we adopted the novel idea of
multifunctional skeletal structures to achieve both humanoid
performance and humanlike proportions. We adopted the
sensor–driver integrated muscle modules for improved muscle
control. In order to demonstrate the effectiveness of these body
structures, we conducted several preliminary movements using
Kengoro.
I. INTRODUCTION
Research for understanding humans through humanoid
development has been widely conducted. ECCE [1] and
anthrob [2] are developed to have humanlike musculoskeletal
structures. We have been developing musculoskeletal hu-
manoids such as Kenta, Kotaro, Kojiro, Kenzoh and Kenshiro
[3]–[7]. Kenshiro is designed with signiﬁcant anatomical
ﬁdelity to humans with respect to not only musculoskeletal
structures, but also muscle arrangement or body proportions.
The research objective of such humanoids is to understand
the human more by developing humanoids with anatomical
ﬁdelity, or achieving several motions in the same way
as humans. We are interested in essential factors required
for humanlike ﬂexibility and skillful motions. We are also
interested in brain mechanism of humans for the ability of
adaptive action acquisition. In order to understand humans
more, we believe that the next challenge that we should be
concentrating on is achieving physically interactive actions
with such humanoids. Humans naturally learn and perform
physically interactive actions such as balancing with envi-
ronmental contacts using four limbs coordinately. Human
motions with environmental contact are started in childhood
from standing and moving by grabbing the environment.
Therefore, we believe that the methodology and mechanism
of humans to achieve physically interactive actions is quite
important to understand humans.
On the other hand, in recent humanoid research, the
importance of not only the position control, but also the force

II. O VERVIEW OF KENGORO ’S DESIGN
The name, Kengoro, is a Japanese male name. Kengoro
is represented with three Chinese characters: Ken, go, and
ro. Each character means tendon, ﬁve and traditional name
ending of Japanese male, respectively. The whole body CAD
image of Kengoro and joint DOFs conﬁguration are shown
in Fig.2. The system architecture of Kengoro is shown in
Fig.3. USB is adopted for the internal communication that
is available easily by connecting to a PC.
In body design of Kengoro, we incorporate the knowledge
of human anatomy in terms of the following four points,
as used in the design process of our previous humanoid
Kenshiro.
• Human mimetic body proportions
• Function replicated skeletal structures
• Anatomically correct muscle arrangement
• Human inspired joint properties

C. Battery system
The battery system is composed of three types of batteries
as shown in Fig.5, i.e., internal PC power, logic power, and
servo power. We prepared four LiFe batteries in total to ﬁt
into the bone shape. Two servo batteries (26.4V , 2500mAh

battery is installed on the back. Runtimes of these batteries
while standing are approximately 22 min for the logic, and
71 min for the servo and 7 hours for the PC.

D. Components inside head
The components that are inside the head of Kengoro
are shown in Fig.6. There are two mini PCs (Intel NUC
i3, D34010WYK), a stereo camera (playstation camera), a
speaker, a reservoir tank for water cooling, a network hub
and an IMU board. One of the PCs is involved in motion
control that manages each muscle information and controls
entire body. The other is involved in vision processing. From
the viewpoint of human mimetics, human brain has a role-
sharing mechanism, having a motor area and a visual area.
By equipping several PCs inside the head, the information
processing load of each PC is reduced. The IMU inside the
head is also based on a human mimetic idea that represents
the semicircular canals of humans.

III. S KELETAL STRUCTURE OF KENGORO
In the skeletal design of Kengoro, we concentrated on the
concept of multifunctional skeletal structures. This implies
that the skeletal structures are equipped with not only the
weight support function, but also other functions for func-
tional high density humanoid systems. We also concentrated
on increasing the number of whole body DOFs further
for higher environmental adaptivity. The machine cutting
spine and foot skeletal structures are new attempts in the
development of musculoskeletal humanoids.
A. Multifunctional skeletal structure
In musculoskeletal humanoids with human mimetic slim
body proportions, there is a trade-off between limited design
space due to the human mimetic proportions and the number
of components for improving humanoid performance. In
order to solve this problem, the idea of multifunctional
skeletal structure is adopted in the skeletal design of Ken-
goro, which is inspired from the multifunctionality of hu-
man biometric components such as bones and muscles. We
integrated power supply or heat dissipation functions into
the skeletal structure. In a related research, a pneumatic
humanoid BuEnwa was developed whose skeletal structure
was made by integrating air tanks with bones [14]. HyQ2Max
[15] has the integrated hydraulic actuation system with a 3D
printed hydraulic manifold.
1) Battery-embedded skeletal structure: In order to
achieve stand-alone motions in the outdoor environment,
we embedded batteries into the skeletal structure of the
upper and lower legs (Fig.5). The leg structure is thin-walled
hollow shaped, and made of CFRP . By taking advantage
of the ﬂexibility in terms of shape of CFRP , tunnels for
electrical cables can be formed so that the system is stable
by protecting the cable from outer disturbances. In pelvis
and femur (upper leg) links, we adopted pitch CFRP that
have high thermal conductivity and adopted the close-contact
element arrangement that places elements (actuators, skeletal
structures, batteries, and boards) in contact. It enables to
transfer heat accumulation between each element.
2) Skeletal structure with artiﬁcial perspiration: For the
upper arm design, we integrated cooling functions into the
skeletal structure (Fig.7). A more detailed description is
provided in [16]. As the cooling mechanism, we constructed
a water pipe integrated structure by 3D printed aluminum
using powder sintered method. We also adopted latent heat
mechanism using bi-layer porous inspired by human perspi-
ration. Cooling water is directly connected to the bi-layer
porous part of the arm and evaporated there. Bi-layer porous
is used in four parts of the arm except for the contact surface
to motors and connection points to other parts.

B. Human mimetic multi-DOFs skeletal structure
1) S-shaped spine structure using machine cutting
springs: In the spine structure of Kengoro, humanlike S-
shaped multiple spine structure is achieved by classifying
24 vertebrae of humans into three parts: cervical, thoracic,
and lumber. Each part is made with appropriate production
method to satisfy each required function (Fig.8). For a cervix
with seven vertebrae, we made them by combination of pull
spring and welding to make it smaller. For thoracic with
12 vertebrae, we made them to be passively movable for
more ﬂexibility in the chest part by making them by cutting
metal. Although our previous spine structure in Kenshiro has
ﬁxed thoracic vertebrae for protecting the inner components,
such as motors, boards and electronic cables, Kengoro’s six
out of 12 thoracic vertebrae from the top have movable
range in the pitch direction for more anatomical ﬁdelity.
The number of rib bones is 12 which is the same as in
human. Each bone connects to each vertebra at the back
side and to the sternum in the front side. The rib is made

upper body weight is achieved.
2) Human mimetic foot structure with multi-DOFs and
multi-sensors: For the foot structure of Kengoro, we did not
adopt a plate-foot that is often used in ordinary humanoid,
but the multi-DOF foot, which replicates the multibone
connected structure of humans, since we believe that the
end effector ﬂexibility is important in the situation of envi-
ronmental contact. A more detailed description is provided
in [17]. The foot is composed of mainly three parts, which
are toe, ankle and heel, and is connected by spring-shaped
elastic elements (3-DOFs). The total DOFs of the foot is 16
including ﬁve ﬁngers that have two rotational DOFs each and
the two spring-shaped elements with three DOFs. The toe is
actuated by a muscle wire connected to the muscle actuator
(leg muscle #27 in Table IV) that is installed on the tibia link
(lower leg). In order to prevent the foot from disassembling
or unexpected deformation, constraint for the multi-DOFs
have to be considered. Inspired from human foot constraint,
we attach a visco-elastic element (urethane rubber) at the sole
replicating the human plantar aponeurosis and skin functions.
It passively constrains the foot and enable adaptation to the
environment. The 12 load cells (an axis force sensor) for
measuring the distributed ground reaction forces are installed
on each foot. The location of each sensor is as follow: ﬁve
for each tip toe, ﬁve for the base of ﬁnger and two for the
heel.
IV . M USCLE STRUCTURE AND CONTROL OF KENGORO
A. Sensor-driver integrated muscle module
For the muscle actuator of Kengoro, the sensor-driver
integrated muscle module [18] that enables ﬂexible tension
control is used (Fig.10). The muscle module is composed of
a motor, motor driver, tension sensor, and thermal sensor
and is covered by sheet metals. Owing to constitute the
whole body with the modules, all the muscles of Kengoro
(116 muscles) can be controlled by tension. The module
improved the system’s reliability by solving the following
problems: secular change of tension value, exposure of
electrical components (connectors and cables), and velocity
based tension control.
The number of muscle modules in upper body are as
follow: ten for neck, eight for spine, four for blade, six for
shoulder, ﬁve for elbow (including pronation and supination),
three for wrist, and three for ﬁngers. For the legs, seventeen
for hip, ten for knee, seven for ankle, one for foot ﬁngers.

C. Muscle cover made of spring and forming materials
Flexible outer cover is important for motions involving
environmental contact. Muscle cover that is made of soft
forming materials is adopted for Kengoro by replacing mus-
cle sleeve used in Kenshiro. Fig.11 shows the muscle cover
used in Kengoro. Inserting a low stiffness spring into the
muscle cover prevents the muscle wires from being pressed
in the cover. Changing the muscle exterior to soft material
is effective for musculoskeletal humanoid because the ratio
of muscle surface in the whole body is dominant in such
humanoid.

V. P RELIMINARY MOVEMENTS USING KENGORO
As veriﬁcation of Kengoro’s body performance, several
preliminary movements are demonstrated (Fig.13).
A. V eriﬁcation of multifunctional skeletal structures
In order to achieve a humanoid that can move in real
world, outdoor capability is necessary. We conducted a
stand-alone movement in real world for the veriﬁcation of
the internal battery system of Kengoro. In the movement,
Kengoro’s arms are moved while maintaining its balance. In
the push up movement, we demonstrated the upper body
performance of Kengoro, in which the arms are able to
support its weight owing to a new cooling method. From
these preliminary movements, we conﬁrmed that the body
design approach of the multifunctional skeletal structure is
effective because it improves humanoid performances while
maintaining human mimetic proportions.
B. Motions with environmental contact
For the motions in contact with surroundings, roll over
motion and steps with wall support is achieved. During
motions with environmental contact, adaptive behavior by
ﬂexible muscle control or joint ﬂexibility is necessary to
avoid breaking of the body and joints. Those demonstrations
are achieved by whole body ﬂexibility owing to tension con-
trol in all muscles, muscle cover for cushion and humanlike
large DOFs in the whole body.
VI. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, we described the concept and speciﬁcation
of human mimetic musculoskeletal humanoid, Kengoro. The
design concept of Kengoro is based on, both, the human
mimetic humanoid design approach that was adopted in our
predecessor humanoid Kenshiro, and body characteristics to
achieve physically interactive actions in the real world. In
order to achieve physically interactive actions, all muscle
actuators in the whole body is composed of the sensor-
driver integrated muscle modules that are capable of ﬂexible
tension control. Furthermore, the effectiveness of the idea
of multifunctional skeletal structure is demonstrated from
both aspects of keeping human mimetic body proportions and
improving performance of Kengoro that can achieve pushing
up or motions in the outside.

As part of future studies, we would develop software sys-
tems for human mimetic musculoskeletal humanoids. We are
interested in a control system not only for the muscle layer,
but also for the joint and end effector layers for adaptive
whole body control. Utilization of toe DOF in the motions
or environmental perception using distributed force sensors
on the sole are also interesting research topics. Furthermore,
the method of environmental contact detection using whole
body human mimetic joint DOFs are necessary for physically
interactive actions. Whole body balance control involving
COG control is also required for such humanoids in which
joint softness is inherent. By implementing those software
systems, we would like to achieve motions utilizing its
human mimetic body characteristics as humans do.
