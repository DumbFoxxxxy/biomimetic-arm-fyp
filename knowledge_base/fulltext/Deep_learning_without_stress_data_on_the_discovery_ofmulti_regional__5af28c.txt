Deep learning without stress data on the discovery of multi-regional
hyperelastic properties
Ruike Shi 1 · Haitian Yang 1 · Jianxu Chen 2 · Klaus Hackl 3 · Stéphane Avril 4 · Yiqian He 1,3
Received: 20 May 2024 / Accepted: 22 December 2024 / Published online: 28 January 2025

Abstract
Constitutive material modeling is an important basis for mechanical analysis. Machine learning methods based on neural
networks have been extensively used for the discovery of material constitutive laws. Recently, Flaschel et al. (Comput Methods
Appl Mech Eng 381:113852, 2021) proposed a new “unsupervised” framework that does not need any stress labels, which
are usually difﬁcult to measure in experiments. However, there is no work on the application of the unsupervised machine
learning models for multi-regional constitutive models of heterogeneous materials. To address this issue, this paper develops
a new unsupervised multi-regional constitutive learning model. In the implementation of this machine learning model, two
indicators, i.e., the residual nodal force indicator and the free energy indicator, are ﬁrst deﬁned to identify nonhomogeneous
interfaces or material distributions. Then, the combined identiﬁcation of multi-regional constitutive models is conducted by
adding a switch function to the extended strain invariants layer of the neural network. Finally, multi-regional constitutive
models can be trained in one neural network at the same time. The effectiveness of the proposed model is veriﬁed through
numerical simulation examples including an application to constitutive modeling of soft biological tissues undergoing regional
damage.
Keywords Machine learning · Hyperelastic constitutive models · Heterogeneous materials · Unsupervised model ·
Multi-regional distribution
1 Introduction
Constitutive models describe the mechanical behavior of
a given material through mathematical equations [ 1], and
the constitutive material modeling is critical for computa-
tional analyses of structures, particularly in characterizing
and modeling complex materials.
Traditionally, constitutive modeling resorts to calibrating
unknown material parameters of a phenomenological model

the ﬁnite element model updating (FEMU) [ 2] or the virtual

based on machine learning have been rapidly developed and
shown signiﬁcant advantages compared to classical material
models. The key idea of these machine learning approaches
is to learn the relationships between strains and stresses, and
provide surrogates for these relationships using neural net-
works [4–10]. However, a drawback of these approaches is
their requirement for a large amount of stress labels (so-called
supervised learning in machine learning), which are very
difﬁcult to measure in experiments and can be computation-
ally expensive in numerical simulations [ 11].
To overcome the limitations of the supervised data-driven
methods, the Efﬁcient Unsupervised Constitutive Law Iden-
tiﬁcation and Discovery (EUCLID) was recently proposed
by Flaschel et al. [ 11, 12] and Thakolkaran et al. [ 13].
The EUCLID employs sparse regression [ 11, 12] or neu-
ral networks (NN) [13] to generate constitutive laws and can

automatically discover generalized standard material mod-
els with realistically measurable data, whether it is deﬁned
by the Helmholtz free energy or the dissipation potential
[12]. The EUCLID is an unsupervised method that does
not rely on stress data but it utilizes global reaction forces
and full-ﬁeld displacement data from DIC or DVC instead.
This method has been demonstrated for various constitutive
models, including hyperelasticity [ 11, 13], rate-independent
plasticity [ 14], elastodynamics [ 15], viscoelastic [ 16], and
the application for human brain tissue [ 17]. Notably, these
previous studies have focused mainly on the discovery of a
single model for homogeneous materials.
The development of neural network for multi-regional
constitutive models has important applications as heteroge-
neous materials widely exist. For instance, soft biological
tissues are usually heterogeneous [ 18], for example, arte-
rial wall is a three-layers structure with different constitutive
models in each layer [ 19, 20]. Therefore, it is of much
interest to develop unsupervised machine learning meth-
ods for constitutive modeling of heterogeneous materials
when their components could have different constitutive
models and material properties. Generally, the existing of
multi-regional constitutive models will make the machine
learning more complex than homogeneous materials in two
aspects. Firstly, although required, material distributions are
unknown. Secondly, combined learning increases the com-
plexity and computational cost of neural networks. However,
to the best of the authors’ knowledge, there is still no work
on the application of unsupervised deep learning method for
constitutive modeling of heterogeneous materials.
Therefore, inspired by the scope of the NN-EUCLID [13],
a new machine learning model is further developed spe-
cially for discovering multi-regional constitutive models of
heterogeneous materials in this article. The proposed model
includes two main steps: (1) Firstly, in order to ﬁnd the hetero-
geneous material distribution, we deﬁne two indicators, i.e.,
the residual nodal forces and the free energy, which are used
with full-ﬁeld measurements together to identify the inter-
face of different materials and material distribution region,
respectively; (2) Secondly, when the heterogeneous material
distribution is found, a switch function can be deﬁned to allo-
cate any spatial point to a speciﬁc material component, and
then a new neural network can be built, considering regional
heterogeneities. In this neural network, cloned neurons in the
ﬁrst layer are used to train multiple constitutive models and
the switch function is added into these neurons to allocate
any neuron to a speciﬁc region.
The paper is organized as follows: In Sect. 2, the basic
framework of the unsupervised deep learning method is
introduced and the multiple constitutive modeling based on
unsupervised deep learning method is presented. Section 3
gives the identiﬁed results of several numerical examples in
order to verify the effectiveness of the proposed approach.
Section 4 concludes the paper and describes possible appli-
cations for future work.

2.1.2 Unsupervised deep learning of constitutive models
The basic procedure of the NN-EUCLID is illustrated in
Fig. 2. Firstly, full-ﬁeld displacements and global reaction
forces are recorded during quasi-static deformation of a
hyperelastic specimen experiment. Secondly, the strain ﬁelds
are generated in a ﬁnite element mesh from the full-ﬁeld dis-
placement. Thirdly, an ensemble of ICNN-based constitutive
models transforms strain ﬁelds into stress ﬁelds, and these
stress ﬁelds are used to calculate internal and external nodal
forces. Finally, constitutive models are iteratively trained to
minimize the loss function based on the conservation of lin-
ear momentum.

2.2 Multiple constitutive models trained by NN
In this section, we extend the unsupervised machine learning
method in Sect. 2.1 to handle material heterogeneity. The key
idea of the proposed model is ﬁrstly to ﬁnd the heterogeneous
material distribution and divide the entire domain into multi-
regional subdomains with their own constitutive models and
material properties, and ﬁnally identify these multi-regional
constitutive models in one modiﬁed ICNN.

4 Conclusion and outlook
In recent years, signiﬁcant progress has been made in the
development of an unsupervised framework proposed by
Flaschel et al. [ 11] and S. Kumar et al. [ 13] for identifying
constitutive models and parameters using neural networks.
This framework eliminates the need for stress data and
prior knowledge of the speciﬁc form of constitutive mod-
els during the machine learning process. However, whilst
previous studies have mainly focused on identiﬁcation of sin-
gle constitutive model in homogeneous materials, this paper
introduces a new unsupervised deep learning method specif-
ically designed for heterogeneous materials.
The unsupervised machine learning system proposed in
this article has two steps. Firstly, two material distribution
indices are deﬁned to identify non-homogeneous material
interfaces. Speciﬁcally, two indicators are deﬁned includ-
ing the residual nodal force and element-wise free energy.
The essential idea is to use the imbalance on the material
interface when both the actual deformation ﬁeld in hetero-
geneous material and the homogenized constitutive model
123

===== PAGE 29 =====
Computational Mechanics (2025) 76:117–146 145
are used. Secondly, a new NN is established by extending
neurons in the ﬁrst layers, utilizing switch function at the
basic layer of the neural network. This enables the identi-
ﬁcation of multi-region constitutive models by considering
the spatial relationships between different material regions.
The effectiveness of the proposed model is validated through
numerical examples. In the ﬁrst example, the focus is on
regional constitutive identiﬁcation using full-ﬁeld displace-
ment data generated from materials with spatially varying
constitutive parameters, as well as when multiple material
combinations exist within the domain. The proposed method
demonstrates accurate energy and stress identiﬁcation, even
in situations where the speciﬁc distribution and form of
constitutive models are unknown. Furthermore, this paper
explores the application of the proposed method in identify-
ing constitutive models for irregular inclusion embedded in
layered materials. While the residual nodal force indicator
may be sensitive to the threshold and the energy indicator
may produce some errors in region identiﬁcation due to the
use of triangular elements, both indicators can approximately
determine the material distribution or interface. Finally, the
potential application of the proposed method to identify the
post-damage mechanical behavior of soft tissues was shown.
In the above all examples, it is shown that both the residual
nodal force and energy indicators can generally ﬁnd material
distribution well, although the performance energy indicator
could be sensitive with the use of traction boundary condi-
tions and inner complex shape of inclusions or voids.
A limitation of the work is the requirement of full-ﬁeld
measurement information, which could be difﬁcult to obtain
in some cases, e.g., the inner point of living tissue or three-
dimensional samples. In future work, the possibility to use
reduced measurement input will be studied, for instance,
e.g., only the deformation on boundary or material inter-
faces. This will generalize the applicability of the proposed
method easier to apply in real problems. Another limitation
of this work is that there is a need of sharp boundary between
two regions with different properties, therefore some special
methods or simpliﬁcations are required for smooth transition
between two materials. Another main limitation of the pro-
posed method is the lack of application to real data. Now that
the method has been introduced and assessed with simulated
data, this will be considered in future work, with challenges
related to experimental bias and other uncertainty and imper-
fections. Besides, some unsupervised learning approaches,
like PCA (Principal component analysis) [ 37], will be con-
sidered in our future work. In these methods, the training
data consists of a set of inputs without any corresponding
targets [38], and usually a low dimensional representation is
achieved to improve the computational efﬁciency [ 39].
In summary, this paper presents a new unsupervised deep
learning method for heterogeneous materials, addressing
the challenges of regional and heterogeneous constitutive
identiﬁcation. The proposed method has numerous poten-
tial applications in the ﬁeld of biomedical engineering. For
instance, the proposed method can be used directly to con-
struct a multi-regional constitutive model based on medical
images of soft tissue, like the arterial inner wall or normal
tissue with tumors, thus the mechanical stress–strain rela-
tionships for all the components of tissues can be obtained
together in NN formulas without any assumption of classic
hyperelastic models. Moreover, these identiﬁed constitutive
models can be conveniently applied into numerical simula-
