Development of Musculoskeletal Humanoid Kenzoh with Mechanical
Compliance Changeable Tendons by Nonlinear Spring Unit
Y uto Nakanishi, Tamon Izawa, Masahiko Osada, Nobuyuki Ito, Shigeki Ohta, Junichi Urata and Masayuki Inaba
Abstract— We have developed and studied musculoskeletal
humanoids. Our goal is to achieve more human-like humanoids
which can do natural and dynamic motions as well as humans.
Especially, joint DOFs redundancy, mechanical joint compli-
ance and great joint generating power are very important
for humanlike dynamic motions and tasks, such as running,
jumping, landing, throwing/kicking/catching a ball, and so on.
This paper presents the concepts and aim of this project and
also shows the outline of our latest results about development of
new musculoskeletal humanoid Kenzoh, which is the succeeding
version of our previous Kojiro.
I. INTRODUCTION
If humanoids’ bodies become as ﬂexible and complicated
as human beings, they will be able to do more natural and
various motions than conventional humanoids[1], [2], [3],
which have less joint DOFs and a rigid body than human
beings. Based on these standpoints, we have developed
the fully tendon-driven redundant humanoids, which have
spine structures like a human body, and studied on how to
control such a complicated body through the relationship
between actuator outputs and sensor inputs [4]. And also we
developed the humanoid Kojiro[5], which has both redundant
and powerful actuators in order to achieve fullbody motions,
and we managed to let Kojiro balance using spines[6] and
walk using simple reﬂex[7].
Then, the next motivation of our groups is to let muscu-
loskeletal humanoids do more dynamic motions with some
hard contact to environments like sports as shown in Fig.1. In
this paper, we describe development of a new musculoskele-
tal humanoid, which is named Kenzoh, as a new adaptive
humanoid platform and also our future sports’ partner. The
challenging points of this robot are following:
∙ More similar humanlike body structure: arrange-
ments of multi-joints’ DOFs and redundant muscles
∙ Joint softness by mechanical stiffness adjustment
muscle modules for absorption of impact
∙ More joint power generating high torque and high
speed for sports activities
II describes characteristics of Kenzoh focusing on these
three points and comparison with other robots researches.
III describes key technologies in Kenzoh to achieve these
three points. Finally in IV, we conﬁrmed performances of

II. C HARACTERISTICS OF KENZOH
A. Redundancy of joints’ DOFs
In order to implement enough joints’ DOFs, Kenzoh has
a spine structure which consists of three spherical joints, a
neck structure which consists of three spherical joints, and
a shoulder structure which consists of a collarbone and a
scapula like human beings as shown in Fig.2. Quantity of
joint DOFs of Kenzoh is more than general anthropomorphic
robots, and also it almost equals to quantity of joint DOFs
in Table I, which is upper body data because Kenzoh is
only upper body at present. In Table I, Pneuborn-7II is
musculoskeletal infant robot by Narioka et al. [8], ECCE
is life-sized musculoskeletal humanoid by Marques et al.[9]
and Kojiro is our developed previous musculoskeletal hu-
manoid. There are some blanks in this table, because some
speciﬁcation of another group’s robot cannot be surveyed
from published papers.

consisting of many bones, we and another group to develop

B. Soft joint by mechanical stiffness adjustment muscle
In tendon-driven robots, mechanical joint stiffness can be
easily controlled by setting nonlinear spring unit modules
both antagonistic muscles [10], [11] like in Fig.3. A nonlin-
ear spring unit, which we call NST in this paper, is the unit
which has nonlinear relationship between tension and spring
constant of the tendon. The larger tension becomes, the larger
spring constant becomes. NST can be easily implemented as

a simple structure consisting of one spring and guided pulleys
of tendon(wire) like in Fig.4.
If robot’s joint is adjusted as low stiffness by NST, it
can be adapted against suddenly collision with environments
without no joint feedback control, because low joint stiffness
is mechanically achieved by NST units. Of course, low joint
stiffness can be achieved by joint feedback control by joint
torque sensor[12](or tension sensor in case of tendon robots).
However, it is difﬁcult to achieve enough fast response to
impulse force, and also it does not work by some failure of
a torque sensor, which often is fragile.
Therefore, in order to develop adaptive humanoids with
environment as our sports’ partners, robot’s joints should be
designed to control its own joint stiffness mechanically. One
of most important Kenzoh characteristics is that Kenzoh’s
all muscles have NST units , so Kenzoh’s all joints stiffness
can be adjusted mechanically.
C. Joint power for supporting robot’s own weight
Fig.5 shows the arrangement of tendon paths of Ken-
zoh. Kenzoh have many muscles which are implemented
as winding wire mechanism by a motor with a pulley.
Kenzoh uses two type of motor(MAXON 90W AC motor
and MAXON 40W AC motor). Kenzoh’s upper body has
totally 3900[W] PWR, which almost equals to full body
total PWR of Kojiro, which is the previous musculoskeletal
humanoid we developed. Therefore, Kenzoh has as about
twice joint power as Kojiro, which seems to be enough to
support Kenzoh’s own weight by its arm.
1) Light weight ends of arms and legs: By arranging
almost all muscles to actuate spines and shoulder joints in
chest bone, light weight hands and foots can be designed.
For example, Kenzoh’s radius has no motor units to actuate
its wrist joint, because all motor units for wrist are put in
upperarm structure. In case of Kenzoh’s leg, actuators for its
ankle joint can be put in its lowerleg bone, actuators for
its knee joint can be put in its thigh bone and actuators
for its hip joint can be put in its pelvis bone, which is
enough space because it has no actuators for spine joints,
which are almost put in its chest bone. The lighter ends of
arms and legs become, the more payloads of arms and legs
become and the faster speed of hands and foots becomes,
which leads more powerful and dynamic performance of
humanoids. Thus strategy of arrangement muscles is similar
to human beings.
2) Humanlike shoulder and forearm structure of Kenzoh:
Kenzoh’s shoulder structure consists of collarbone, scapula

D. Simultaneous implementation of joint softness, redun-
dancy and powerful performance
It is not difﬁcult to satisfy each demand(i.e. joint soft-
ness, redundancy and powerful performance) individually
and many researches about each demand have been studied.
However, it is very difﬁcult to satisfy simultaneously all
three demands because of limitation of body space .W e
have to manage to stuff many and various elements, such
as powerful muscle actuator units, nonlinear spring tension
units and motor driver boards as shown in Fig.7.
For example, newly DLR JUSTIN[13] has mechanical
joint stiffness control modules in all joints and generates
enough joints torque, but redundancy of joint DOFs is less
than Kenzoh. Musculoskeletal infant robot Pneuborn-7II[8]
has ﬂexible pneumatic actuators with mechanical elasticity,
however, it has less joint DOFs and less actuators. Pneumatic
actuator’s power depends on accumulator’s pressure and
valve’s diameter. In order to improve actuator power, valve’s
size has to be bigger. Therefore, pneumatic actuators may
not be ﬁt for embedding numerous actuator systems. Also
very humanlike musculoskeletal legs[14] and a shoulder[15]
with redundant pneumatic actuators have been developed,
however, these robots are implemented as only several parts
of fullbody robot. By these pneumatic actuators, it seems to
be difﬁcult to develop fullbody powerful humanoids with
redundant joints and muscles. WBD-II[] has joint-driven
articulated spine joints and enough power to walk and dance,
but it has no mechanisms to adjust joint stiffness and its
arm joint structure is quite simpler than human’s. Human
mimetic humanoid ECCE[9] and our developed Kojiro adopt
the same tendon-driven actuator systems as Kenzoh, and also
have almost the same redundancy of joint DOFs as Kenzoh.
However, these robots do not have enough joint power and
mechanisms to adjust joint stiffness.
In order to achieve simultaneous implementation of joint
softness, redundancy and powerful performance, it is very
important to make each module as small and high efﬁciency
as possible and to design bone structure to stuff many
elements keeping easiness of maintenance. In next session,
we introduce key technologies to do this simultaneous im-
plementation using in Kenzoh.

III. K EY TECHNOLOGIES OF KENZOH
A. Implementation of nonlinear spring tension unit
Kenzoh adopts two types of NST unit developed by
ourselves. One is the integration type with actuator unit
parts as shown in Fig.8. Advantage of this NST unit is to
utilize effectively motor shaft space as spring and guided
pulley of NST units, which often becomes dead space
in previous musculoskeletal humanoids we developed. In
Kenzoh, the integration NST units are mainly used in a
chest bone, bladebones and upperarms, which have enough
spaces. The other NST is the rotary NST unit as shown in
Fig.9. Advantage of this NST unit is to put it anywhere
through its tendon path, which enables to widen freedom
of robot design. It is very useful at designing a complicated
structures of Kenzoh which has many elements. And also
this rotary NST unit can achieve higher stiffness than other
NST mechanisms[16]. In Kenzoh, the rotary NST units are
mainly used in a spine vertebrae, which have less spaces.
B. Highpower , small and robust motor driver board
We newly develop a distributed motor driver board
for Kenzoh’s muscles[17] in Fig.10. This board size is
26x46[mm], which is almost half size of the previous motor
board(H8-15D) used in Kojiro and also it has more robust
against motor servo noise, thanks to separating a logic power

C. Bone design to accumulate many module elements
Kenzoh has many elements, especially, chest has to embed
many elements, that is, 26 muscle modules(20 of 26 muscle
modules are integration units with NST) and 26 motor driver
boards. In order to pack modules more effectively, these
modules are put in a line to the center of the chest bone
structure. However, it leads bad maintenance to put modules
in the center of chest, because it is difﬁcult to access centered
modules easily. Therefore, Kenzoh’s chest structure consists
of three parts, that is a front part, a center part and a back
part, as shown in Fig.11. And also each part is designed
so as not to depend on each other, the motor test of each
part can be checked individually, which enables to do easy
maintenance.

IV . E XPERIMENTS IN KENZOH PERFORMANCES
At this time, Kenzoh’s head, spine, right arm already
have developed as shown in Fig.12. Left arm is now under
construction, so we set dummy left arm, whose weight is
equal to the weight of real Kenzoh’s right arm.
We carried out some experiments, such as Kenzoh arm’s
payload test in Fig.13, which is lifting up motion of 5[kg]
weight, and Kenzoh spine’s movable range test as shown in
Fig.14. In these experiment, all muscles were controlled base
on muscles’ length by motor driver. All muscles’ length order
could be calculated by Kenzoh’s geometric body model in
Fig.5 at each goal joints’ posture.
Fig.15 shows that the estimation of motor core temperature
based on thermal control[18] from motor input current and
temperature of motor’s outer in a FPGA-01D during Fig.13
motion. From this graph, we conﬁrmed that the actuator
Fig. 15. Time transition of input current and estimated motor core
temperature during lifting up motion
systems in Kenzoh can generate enough joint torque during
this motion, because there are some margin in motor’s core
temperature, which is about 60[degree] in this experiment
and the limit of motor’s core temperature is 100[degree].
Fig.16 shows experiment image of shock absorption by
NST mechanisms in abdominal muscles of Kenzoh. This
experiment is a simple example of tackled case in sports ac-
tivities, such as in footballs. In this experiment, we compared
two cases, that is, high stiffness and low stiffness of spine
joints by using NST units. In high stiffness case, spine’s
tendons are all relaxed where NST’s spring displacement
is nearly 0[mm], which is initial position. In low stiffness
case, on the other hand, spine’s tendons are all tightened
where NST’s spring displacement is nearly 35[mm], which
is almost mechanical limitation position.
Fig.17 shows the result of this experiment. The upper
graph is in the case of high stiffness and the lower graph
is in the case of low stiffness. As you can see this graph,
the peak of tension of the abdominal muscle in the case of
low stiffness is quite lower than in the case of high stiffness.
And also the impact time distance in the low stiffness case
is shorter than in the high stiffness case. By this result, we
conﬁrmed that NST’s spring could certainly absorb impact
force at the sudden collision, which can be useful when
robots play some sports.
V. C ONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORKS
This paper proposes how to develop new adaptive hu-
manoid platform which can do more dynamic motion with
hard contact to environments like sports activities, and shows
a new musculoskeletal humanoid Kenzoh actually developed
as an implementation of this design concept, which is si-
multaneous realization of joints’ softness, redundancy and
powerfule performance.
As future works, we plan Kenzoh to catch a football using
spine joints and whole arm joints. In catching a football, it
seems to be very important to absorb ball’s contact impact
