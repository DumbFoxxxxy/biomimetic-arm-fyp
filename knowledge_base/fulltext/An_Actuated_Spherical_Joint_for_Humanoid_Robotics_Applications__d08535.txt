An Actuated Spherical Joint for Humanoid Robotics
Applications
Michele Folgheraiter *, Sharafatdin Y essirkepov, Alikhan Y essaly
* Department of Robotics and Mechatronics, School of Engineering and Digital Sciences, Nazarbayev University
Nur-Sultan, Kazakhstan
michele.folgheraiter@nu.edu, shyessirkepov@gmail.com, alikhan.yessaly@nu.edu.kz
Abstract—A newly developed actuated spherical joint is intro-
duced for application in the ﬁeld of humanoid robotics. The mech-
anism consists of a parallel structure with four limbs in a 3PSS-1S
kinematic conﬁguration. This, allows to perform rotations with
respect to any arbitrary axis passing through the center of the
joint, effectively implementing a spherical motion. By having the
actuation system located on the ﬁxed platform allows maximizing
the payload and reducing the energy consumption. The solution of
the inverse kinematics is provided together with simulation results
conducted in V-REP . It is shown how parameters like the moving
base diameter and the links length inﬂuence the workspace of
the mechanism. A ﬁrst prototype is presented together with
preliminary results showing a rotation range of ±45 ° about
the pitch and roll axes and 120° about the yaw axis.
Index Terms—Actuated Spherical Joint, 3PSS-1S Parallel Ma-
nipulator, Humanoid Robotics.
I. I NTRODUCTION
Humanoid robotics aims at developing anthropomorphic
robots able to move and manipulate objects in a similar way
humans can do. Thanks to their morphology and kinematic
architecture, this new class of machines will extend the ca-
pabilities of classical manipulators and mobile robots to the
household and the public environments, i.e. enhancing the life
quality for human beings during the course of this century [1].
Most of the humanoid robots currently developed by re-
search centers and companies integrate articulated limbs based
on sequences of rotational joints. This is due to different
reasons: wide availability in the market of different type of
rotational servomotors, well understood kinematic architecture
(DK modeling, IK solutions, workspace analysis, singular
conﬁguration, etc.), straight forward mechanical design and
control.
However, if compared with parallel one, open kinematics
chains have also disadvantages. The backlash of the differ-
ent joints sum up reducing the overall robot precision and
repeatability. To reduce the mechanical complexity, in most
of the robots, the actuator that move the correspondent joint
is displaced in its proximity. This has the effect to increase
the load of the joints that are located at the beginning of the
kinematic chain, effectively reducing the overall end-effector
payload and increasing its energy consumption.
In order to partially solve these problems, a possibility is
to substitute the sequences of three revolute joints having
orthogonal axes with a single actuated spherical joints. As it is
shown in Fig 1 we can think to upgrade the revolute pitch-roll-
yaw joints of the shoulders and the hips with spherical joints.
To notice that even though the two kinematic architectures
have the same DOFs, they are not equivalent. While the second
one allows to rotate about any arbitrary axis passing through
the center of the spherical joint, in the ﬁrst one, when the
pitch-axis aligns with the yaw-axis, one DOF is lost and some
rotations are not possible.
Fig. 1. Kinematic comparison between: a) humanoid robot with only revolute
(r) joints and b) humanoid robot with spherical (s) joints. In b), the sequences
of three revolute joins with orthogonal axes are substituted with a single
spherical joint.
To implement an actuated spherical joint different solutions
were developed. In [2], [3] the shoulder of an anthropomorphic
artiﬁcial arm is moved by ﬁve McKibben artiﬁcial muscles.
Three are used in a parallel conﬁguration to perform the
orientation of the upper arm, and the other two to rotate
it about its current direction. A similar concept is the one
introduced in the humanoid torsos presented in [4] and [5],
where the spherical joints are actuated by tendons attached
to pulleys rotated by servomotors. This has the advantage to
exploit precise and widely available actuators in order to move
truly anthropomorphic kinematic architectures.
In [6] and [7] the parallel spherical manipulator has three
limbs with a RRR kinematic structure. The moving platform is
actuated thanks to three rotational servomotors which output
shaft is connected to the ﬁrst joint of each limb. The ﬁrst
and second link of each limb are connected with joints which
rotational axes form an angle of 90°. Also the actuators’ bodies
form an angle of about 45° with respect the ﬁxed platform.

Another very interesting parallel kinematic structure is the
spherical wrist designed for medical applications presented in
[9]. The 3PSS-1S parallel mechanism includes three lateral
limbs and a central one, however in comparison with [8]
the prismatic joints are displaced orthogonally to the ﬁxed
platform. The non actuated S-joints are realized by connecting
an universal joint and a rotational joint in series, effectively
approximating the motion of a spherical joint.
A different humanoid robotic shoulder concept is introduced
in [10] that consists of two subsystems, a parallel mechanism
resembling the human girdle articulation and a serial one
representing the glenohumeral joint. Interesting is the ﬁrst
kinematic structure that is formed by three lateral limbs with
a SPS architecture and a central one with a PS architecture.
While the lateral legs have the usual function to orientate the
moving platform, the central one has the role to ﬁx the position
of the center of rotation. Thus, allowing a forth translational
DOF. Another combination of parallel and serial kinematic
chain to implement a spherical joint is presented in [11]. In
this case, the parallel mechanism includes only two limbs one
with one link and a RR architecture and the other with two
links and a RRR architecture. This allows the moving platform
to have two rotational DOFs. In order to add the third rotation,
and therefore reproduce a spherical motion, a serial kinematic
chain is connected to the moving platform where its rotational
joint has the axis of rotation orthogonal to the platform.
In this paper we introduce an actuated spherical joint
speciﬁcally designed for humanoid robotics applications. The
3PSS-S kinematic structure includes three lateral limbs having
each two links and a central limb that constrains the moving
platform to a spherical motion. The three prismatic joints that
actuate the mechanism are implemented with lead screws.
These, are connected to rotational motors displaced orthog-
onally to the ﬁxed platform in order to decrease the moving
platform inertia and increase its payload.

II. S PHERICAL JOINT KINEMA TICS
The parallel kinematic architecture of the spherical joint,
see Fig. 2, consists of three lateral limbs having a Prismatic-
Spherical-Spherical (PSS) structure and a central limb which
includes only a spherical joint. Thus, we can label the mech-
anism as a 3PSS-1S parallel manipulator. More speciﬁcally,
each later limb starts with an actuated prismatic joint that
connects the ﬁxed base to the ﬁrst link. The second link is
connected to the ﬁrst one and to the base through non actuated
spherical joints. The central leg connects the ﬁxed base and the
moving base by a spherical joint and constrains it to perform
only spherical motions.

III. M ECHANISM SIMULA TION
Before developing the ﬁnal prototype of the spherical joint
we conducted a series of simulations in V -REP software.
This, with the goal to ﬁnd out how some of the mechanism’s
parameters, such as the links’ length and the ﬁxed and moving
platforms dimensions, inﬂuence its range of motion. Figure 3
shows the model of the spherical joint where: (1) represents
the ﬁxed base. (6) is the moving platform that is connected to
the central limb (8) through the spherical joint (7). (4), (9) and
(10) are the three lateral limbs each having a prismatic joint (2)
and a proximal (3) and distal (5) spherical joints respectively.
Fig. 3. V -REP model of the spherical joint.
In order to simulate a parallel structure in V -REP it is
necessary to carefully deﬁne the relationships between the
different objects present in the scene, i.e. joints and links.
The hierarchy tree of our model, see Fig. 4, starts from the
ﬁxed base which position is ﬁxed with respect to the absolute
reference frame of V -REP . The prismatic joint of the ﬁrst limb
P1 is a child of the ﬁxed platform and it is the parent of the
ﬁrst link L11, which in turns is the parent of the proximal
spherical joint S11. The second link L12 is a child of S11
and it is the parent of the distal spherical joint S12. Finally,
the moving platform is a child of S12 and the parent for the
objects that compose the central leg and the other two limbs.
If we consider the ﬁrst and second limb, see Fig. 4, they form
a closed kinematic chain that starts and ends in the ﬁxed base.
In a similar way, we have also a closed chain if we consider
the ﬁrst limb and the central leg or the ﬁrst limb and the third
leg, etc.
To understand the range of motion of the moving platform,
in V -REP we deﬁned a script that allows to control the
prismatic joints in position within a deﬁned range. How it
is possible to see in Fig. 5 the moving platform rotates
about different direction according to how we move the three
actuated prismatic joints. In general, by moving one or two
prismatic joints at the time we can actuate compound rotations
about the pitch-roll axes (see Fig. 5.a, 5.b and 5.c), while by
moving the three prismatic joints at the same time, speed and

IV . P ROTOTYPE DESIGN AND TESTING
A prototype of the spherical joint was designed in Solid-
Works. As it is possible to see from Fig. 10 the CAD design
includes a circular moving platform at the top that is connected
by four spherical bearings to the proximal links of the four
legs. The prismatic joints are realized with lead screws con-
nected to the output shaft of the motors. Furthermore, to avoid
the rotation of the proximal spherical bearing with respect the
direction of the lead screw, a linear rail is introduced in the
design of each linear joint.

A ﬁrst prototype of the joint was realized by assembling 3D
printed plastic parts and customized metal parts. In particular,
the lead screws and the links were built in stainless steel, while
all the other parts, like the ﬁxed and moving platforms, in PLA
material. For the non actuated spherical joints of the limbs
we used spherical ball bearings that allow rotations about the
pitch and roll axes of ±60° and unlimited rotation about the
yaw-axis. As actuation for the prismatic joints we used 12
V brushed DC motors equipped with magnetic encoder and
gearbox. Each motor is controlled in velocity by a dedicated
A Tmega328 microcontroller and a Pololu TB6612FNG motor
driver. Table I shows the main characteristics of the prototype.
Despite the angular velocity of the joint is currently limited to
30°/s, we estimate that by using brush-less DC motors having
lower gear ratio it will be possible to reach up to 120 °/s. It is
also worth to notice here that, although the actual dimension
of the prototype makes it not suitable to be directly integrated
in a humanoid robot, by using metal or carbon ﬁber parts will
be possible to scale the mechanism to half of its actual size.

We performed several test runs, aiming at achieving the lin-
ear joints range limits and measure the corresponded maximum
rotations about the yaw, pitch and roll axes, see Fig.11. The
commands to the motor controller were sent through the serial
port in a string format, i.e. (Motor id, Direction of Rotation,
Duration of rotation). From our experiments, the rotation about
the yaw-axis is limited to 120°, while the angular range with
respect the pitch and roll axes are both of ±45 °.
If the maximum rotations about the pitch and roll axes are
comparable with the one obtained by simulating the mecha-
nism in V -REP , the rotation about the yaw-axis is smaller. One
reason for this is that the spherical joints used in V -REP do not
have limitation, while the one used in the real prototype are
limited in their range of motion. Furthermore, the way they
are oriented with respect to the moving and ﬁxed platforms
also inﬂuences their effective range of rotation. Thus, causing
a collision with the link depending on the current platform
pose.
V. C ONCLUSION
In this paper we presented a new design for an actuated
spherical joint for humanoid robotics application. The 3PSS-1S
kinematic architecture of the mechanism includes three lateral
limbs integrating an actuated prismatic joint, and a central
limb that constrains the moving platform to a spherical motion.
A solution for the IK is introduced that allows to obtain the
prismatic joint position for a given orientation of the moving
platform.
Simulations conducted in V -REP showed how different
parameters of the mechanism inﬂuence the range of rotation
about the x-y-z axes. In particular the diameter of the moving
platform and the length of the second link effect heavily the
yaw-axis rotation.

A ﬁrst prototype of the actuated spherical joint was realized
using a combination of plastic and metal materials. The ﬁnal
mechanism has a weights of 1.5 kg including the three motors
and can rotate of ±45 ° with respect the pitch and roll
axes and 120° with respect the yaw-axis. This represents an
improvement if compared to the joint presented in [8] where
the range of motion is limited to 20° for the roll and pitch
axes and 30° for the yaw axis. With respect to the spherical
joints introduced in [6] and [7] our solution allows to align
the bodies of the three motors, reducing this way the footprint
of the mechanism.
As a future work, it remains to complete the kinematic
analysis of the spherical joint. In particular, it will be necessary
to solve its direct and differential kinematics and to ﬁnd the
singular conﬁgurations. For example, when the second link of
the limb reaches a pose which projection on the x-y plane is
laying on a line that passes through the center of the central
leg’s spherical joint, the mechanism is locked and can not
rotate about the yaw-axis.
Finally, the dimensions of the current prototype are still too
big to allow its integration in a humanoid robot. By using a
CNC machine we can think to realize all the needed parts in
metal. This way, we can scale the entire design and reduce its
weight and footprint.
