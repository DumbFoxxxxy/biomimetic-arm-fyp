Modelling of the Shoulder Complex and Application to 
the Design of Upper Extremities for Humanoid Robots 
 
Bertrand Tondu 
 
LESIA, Département de Génie Electrique, Institut National de Sciences Appliquées, 
 Campus Universitaire de Rangueil, 31077 Toulouse France

Abstract. The classic industrial robot-arm does not need a 
shoulder in the anatom ical sense : it is m erely placed on a 
rotating trunk. The recent development of humanoid robots in 
which two arms must move laterally to a frame-trunk raises 
the question of integrating anthropomorphic shoulders. A 
rigorous analy sis of the m obility of the so-called human 
shoulder complex is necessary in the design of robot 
shoulders. A kinematic model of the shoulder complex is 
discussed and its m obility derived. The consideration of a 
simple clavicle-based equivalent kinematic model has lead to 
highlight a serial nine d.o.f. anthropomorphic robot structure. 
By considering a fundamental kinematic model of a robot-
arm including a clavicle-like li nk, it is shown how the elbow 
singularity can be limited to a restrictive zone of the 
reachable volume. 
Index Terms - Robot Shoulder, Shoulder Kinematic 
modelling, Robot elbow singularity. 
 
I. INTRODUCTION 
 
The devel opment of hum anoid robot s has renewed an 
interest i n ant hropomorphic st ructures whi ch had been 
neglected until now by Industrial Robotics. This is especially 
true for t he shoul der com plex whi ch appears t o be t he most 
mobile joint of t he hum an body . So-cal led ant hropomorphic 
industrial robots are essentially arms rotating around a cent ral 
axis whose range largely c ontributes to the m achine’s 
mobility. The “shoulder” m otion is reduced to an elevation of 
the arm on these robots, completed by an axial arm rotation in 
the case of 7R redundant arm s. The humanoid robot which 
necessitates the lateral in tegration o f two arm s to its b ust 
cannot use this central m obility typical of industrial robot-
arms. C onsequently i t must i ncrease t he rol e of the shoulder 
which generally becomes a t hree d.o.f. spherical joint linking 
the arm directly to the bust, but at a distance from the hum an 
shoulder complexity. As well emphasized by Rosheim [1] the 
design of robot shoul ders i s a l ess expl ored fi eld. It appears 
especially important in th is n ew research field to tak e 
advantage of B iomechanics anal yses of hum an shoulder 
functioning in t he desi gn of hy per-anthropomorphic robot -
arms. The development of artificial m uscle actuators such as 
McKibben pneumatic muscles and t heir use i n R obotics 
should favour t he emergence of such robot structures [2]-[5]. 
We are of the opinion that available kinematic analyses of the 
shoulder girdle is questionable. Consequently, the conclusions 
of th is m odelling are essen tial in the design of roboticized 
shoulders. Our article is divided into four sections. In the first 
section, we deal with the schematization of jointed body links. 
An extension of the historical Demspter’s notation, seemed to 
us t o be i ndispensable i n cl arifying t he kinematic diagrams, 
and to extend them to multiple joints. In the second section, 
we apply this notation t o t he ki nematic m odelling of t he 
shoulder girdle. In t he t hird sect ion an eval uation of t he 
mobility of the shoulder com plex based on our kinematic 
analysis of the shoulder girdle is given. In the fourth section, 
we co nsider th e p ossibility o f d efining an o pen ch ain 
kinematic model equi valent t o t he hum an shoul der com plex, 
leading us t o em phasize a 9 d.o.f. ki nematic model of the 
human upper ext remity. In t he fi nal section, we analyse the 
interest of a robot-arm including a cl avicular link to limit the 
influence of the well-known “elbow singularity”.

VI. CONCLUSION 
 
The mechanical interpretation of the different joints of the 
shoulder complex leads to a m odelling of the shoulder girdle 
in the form of a closed kinematic chain whose mobility can be 
determined th anks to th e Gr übler-Kutzbach form ula. The 
proof that the number of d.o.f. of t he shoulder griddle is equal 
to two has a double consequence : 
i. It rigorously establishes that the number of d.o.f. of t he 
human arm, hand excepted, is equal to nine, 
ii. It l eads t o approachi ng t he shoulder girdle by a 
kinematic m odel reduced t o one l ink joi ned to the 
sternum by a universal joint – an equivalent clavicle. As a 
consequence, a four-l ink m odel of t he hum an arm – 
clavicle-arm-forearm-hand – em erges, whose appl ication 
to robot-arms design offers a bi omimetic solution to the 
elbow bound limit singularity problem. Humanoid robots 
including clavicle-like l inks coul d t hus i mprove t heir 
ability to make their arms function together.
