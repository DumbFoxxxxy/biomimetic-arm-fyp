The Designs and Motions of a Shoulder Structure with a Wide Ra nge
of Movement Using Bladebone-Collarbone Structures
Yoshinao Sodeyama, Tomoaki Yoshikai, Tamaki Nishino, Ikuo Mizuuchi and Masa yuki Inaba
Abstract— We are trying to develop an innovative hu-
manoid which learns from the structure of a human shoulder,
especially the bladebone and collarbone. We consider that the
bladebone-collarbone structures have the following merits,
1)wide range of movement, 2)having the shoulder structure
built on to the outside of the chest, allowing space for a lot of
parts inside the chest, 3)ﬂexible shoulder like humans. In con-
trast, it is diﬃcult to control the shoulder structure because it
have multiple degrees of freedom and extraordinary physical
constraints. An aim of our research is to create a repeatable
motion generation system of the bladebone-collarbone struc-
tures for real musculo-skeletal humanoid robots. In this paper,
we describe the design process of the bladebone-collarbone
structures for a muscle-driven humanoid robot, and the
simulation system considering geometrical constraints of the
structures. Then, we show a motion experiment using the
shoulder structures of the humanoid, based on the simulation
system we presented.
I. INTRODUCTION
At some future point, it will be usual in our daily life
for humanoid robots to assist humans directly. Then,
humanoids must not be dangerous to humans. It will
be of the utmost necessity that the humanoid robot
has a safe and ﬂexible body structure. The ﬂexibility
of the arms will be especially important, because these
parts will come in direct contact with human bodies and
environments.
From this point of view, we have developed a whole-
type musculo-skeletal humanoid robot named ‘’Kotaro”
[1]. The whole articulated structure of Kotaro is driven
by muscles which consists of chemical ﬁbers, a DC motor,
pulleys, and a tension sensor. It has 91 DOF throughout
the body. It has about 90 motors currently, and it can
have up to 120 motors throughout the body.
A number of groups have been trying to develop
human-form robots with a speciﬁc shoulder structure [2]–
[5]. A control mechanism for a musculo-skeletal arm has
been also researched [6]. Referring to their approach, we
have developed a characteristic type of shoulder structure
with ’bladebones and collarbones’ for ﬂexibility, wide
range of movement, and good spatial eﬃciency. We
showed the ﬁrst prototype of the bladebone-collarbone
structure and explained the eﬀectivity of them for
tendon-driven humanoid robots [7]. Based on the results

II. THE DESIGN OF A SHOULDER WITH
BLADEBONE-COLLARBONE STRUCTURES
A. The Hardware Construction of the Shoulder Struc-
ture
The shoulder structure of Kotaro consist of four bone
parts which are the bladebone, the collarbone, the upper-
arm bone, and the chest (Fig. 3).
The Bladebone and the chest constitute a bone connec-
tion which consists of a sternoclavicular joint (which is
between a chest and a collarbone) and an acromioclavicu-
lar joint (which is between a collarbone and a bladebone).
An upperarm can make a motion by an scapulohumeral
joint (which is between an upper-arm and a bladebone).
In addition, a bladebone can make movements of up-and-
down, backward and forward, rotation direction on the
back chest surface. This structure enables it to generate
a wide range of movements for the upper-arm with a
shoulder motion.
Upperarm-bladebone joints (Scapulohumeral joints) of
Kotaro are ball-and-socket joints, but the socket parts
are designed deeper than that of humans to prevent bone
dislocations and to improve the intensity of the joint
frame. However this limits the range of movement. The
maximum range of movement of it is 90-degree at ﬂexion
of a shoulder when Kotaro uses an upperarm-bladebone
joint only. Typically, the maximum range of movement

The shape of the bladebone can generate eﬀective

It is located on the chest surface, having the shoulder
structure built on to the outside of the chest, allowing
space for a lot of parts inside the chest.
Each bone-parts are designed as the assembled struc-
tures of motors, boards and sensors by using 3D-CAD.
Bladebone parts are designed that have DC-motors
(4.5W x 3, 3.0W x 1), a motor driver board (x 1), a
USB-hub board (x 1) and a sensor board (x 1) inside
it (Fig. 4). Each parts are made of nylon resin by a 3D
modeling machine of Rapid Prototyping SLS (Selective
Laser Sintering) way [10] [11]. The strength of the
material of this is also equivalent to ABS plastics ones.
Collarbones are the only structural links between a
chest and bladebones, and bladebones are held on the
surface of a chest by the smallest number of muscles. As
a result, strong tension is generated to the collarbone
parts. Collarbones are made of alloyed metal material
of bronze and stainless used steel by metal type Rapid
Prototyping system for the intensity of the parts.
Upper-arm parts are designed that can contain DC-
motors (4.5W x 2), a motor driver board (x 1) and a
USB-hub board (x 1) (Fig. 5). These motors are for
driving a elbow joint. The RP frame-parts make a parts
unit for ease of maintenance.
The chest parts are made in three sections. It has four
joints as elements of spine joints. A middle section of the
chest parts is principally for driving shoulder blades. The
middle section of the chest parts is designed so that it can
contain DC-motors (20W x 4, 4.5W x 8, 3W x 6), and

A bladebone needs four muscles to move and it has
four output points of muscles (Fig. 9). Each side of the
two muscles from the bladebone are cross interfering each
other. The upper two points of the bladebone is actuated
to the bottom, and lower two points of the bladebone is
pulled from the topside of the chest. These four muscles
are driven by four motors inside the chest. It can control
the motions of shoulder structure that are up-and-down,
back-and-forward, and rotation direction. In addition,
20W motors inside the chest actuate the front under
point of the bladebone to the topside of the chest, so it
can generate an extra power to lift up and rotate the
bladebone and whole arm parts.
Torsion coil springs, as elastic elements, are putted
on the joint between an upper-arm and a bladebone.
The springs generate power which work for negating the
empty weight of an arm, so it helps the muscles to drive
the arm. In addition to this, joint structures can get the
physical ﬂexibility by using the springs.
III. A MOTION GENERATING SYSTEM ON THE
BASIS OF GEOMETRICAL CONSTRAINT
A. 3-Dimentional Geometrical Model for a Humanoid
Robot
We made a 3-D geometrical model of the humanoid
Kotaro (Fig. 10). The model has the dataset that are the
3D-shape of body structure, muscle arrangements (start
points and end points of all muscles), and elastic element
(rubbers and springs) models of Kotaro. We can compute
approximative muscle lengths from a 3-D geometrical
model by straight-line approximation. In addition, we
can see the stereoscopic positions of the muscles and the
condition of the muscle tensions.
The motion of a humanoid is made by simulation using
the geometrical model which can calculate the muscle
length of the whole body and send the data to the real
humanoid robot.
B. A Collision Detection System between Bladebones
and a Chest
We can get the muscle lengths from the geometrical
model, and send the data to the robot to make a motion.
At this moment, when we compute the muscle lengths
for making some motion about a bladebone-collarbone
structure, we have to consider the physical constraint
between bladebones and a thorax because bladebones
move along a thorax surface. Bladebones don’t enter into
the chest and don’t ﬂoat to the chest too. They are in
contact with at least one point, that we deﬁned. We
checked collision detection between a bladebone and a
chest to identify the position of a bladebone on the chest.
When a bladebone moves, it ﬂoats to the chest. In this
bout, a bladebone is rotated in little increments angle
around the vertical axis of a bladebone-collarbone-joint
until the surface of a bladebone is in touch with the
surface of a chest (Fig. 11).

This system can make motions on the geometrical
model. It makes motions to the real humanoid about

humanoid, so this check system is reliable.
C. A Decision Method for conﬁgurations of the
Bladebones
We simulated motions reaching a hand to a red apple
using a 3D-geometrical model and a collision detection
system. We can solve the inverse kinematics because the
DOF of bladebone-upperarm joints, elbow joints, wrist
joints are 3, 1, 1 in sequence. Here, an end-point position
is decided by solving inverse kinematics from center
points of an apple and a bladebone-upperarm joint.
Motions are conﬁned to two types, rotating motion
and backward-forward motion. It is not diﬃcult motions
when the humanoid does the reaching motion naturally.
Fig. 12 shows a process ﬂowchart of this system. At
this time, a distance between center points of an apple
and a bladebone-upperarm joint is named ’distance-A’. A
distance between center points of a bladebone-upperarm
joint and an end-point position is named ’distance-B’.

If distance-A is shorter than distance-B, the reaching

At each motion, the most suitable position of a
bladebone is got decided by the collision detection system
explained in foregoing subsection. After that, a reaching
motion is generated by solving an inverse kinematics.
IV. A MOTION EXPERIMENT WITH A
HUMANOID ROBOT
A. Motions of Bladebone Based on 3D Geometrical
Model
We can simulate a position of a bladebone on 3D-
geometrical model by the system. From this simulation,
we can make motions of a shoulder structure following
geometrical model by sending the muscle length to a real
robot. This driving system is a reproducible method, if
a ﬁrst position of a bladebone is made to respond to a
ﬁrst position of a bladebone on the geometrical model.
However, we cannot make exactly the same position of
bladebone as the geometrical model, because the powers
of frictional forces between a bladebone and a chest and
force of gravity are not in the geometrical model.
It have about ten degrees error as rotating angle of
a bladebone. We have to make an improvement about
this point. We should improve the structure to reduce
frictional forces between bladebones and the chest as
little as possible, and do a 3D-geometrical simlation with
eﬀects of frictional forces and gravity force. Aside from
this, we can drive bladebones by the teaching muscle
lengths and replaying the lengths way. Though muscles
to drive bladebones and upperarms are twenty so it is
more eﬃcient driving motions used geometrical model.
B. An Experiment of a Tracking and a Reaching Motion
We did an experiment of reaching for an apple motion.
This is explained in the foregoing section and stereo
vision censors (Fig. 13, Fig. 14). A center position
coordinate of an apple is input by vision censors as USB
stereo cameras inside a Kotaro head parts. At ﬁrst, vision
censors pick out red color of an apple within the visual
ﬁeld. Stereo cameras can track it to the center in the
visual ﬁeld, from the information of barycentric position
by color abstraction. Two eyes of Kotaro can rotate to
the direction of convergence. Accordingly, the physical
object exists at intersection point of two sight lines. After
this, the process starts generating an arm motion with
bladebone-collarbone structure.

V. CONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE WORKS
A. Conclusions
Heading towards the next stage, we have developed
the speciﬁc shoulder structure with bladebones and
collarbones and suggested the way to drive this structure.
We proposed the design method of which makes the
best possible use of 3D-CAD and Rapid prototyping,
and realized the unprecedented type shoulder structure
for Kotaro. The merits of using shoulder blades are the
ﬂexibility, wide range of movement, and good spatial
eﬃciency are showed in the motion of Kotaro.
We made a motion generating method using a 3D-
geometrical model and vision sensors. It is an eﬀective
driving system for the multi-degree-of-freedom shoulder
structure which has bladebones making a drive on the
chest surface. The motion generation system was based
on the collision detection between the bladebone and
the chest on the geometrical model, and restrictions of
motion types of the bladebones. Then, we showed an
experiment of the reaching motion with bladebone. The
errors are caused by frictional forces and gravity force but
it can be considered as unimportant factor by generating
a reaching motion with using visual feedbacks.
In this paper, we only made motions restricted to ro-
tation and backward-forward direction only for motions
of bladebones, but we should make more natural driving
system of bladebones coordinated action of arms using
multi-degree-of-freedom to the full. We should consider
for a better search method of bladebones on a chest
surface. As a way, virtual spring models are arranged
on bladebones of a 3D-geometrical model and rotate a
bladebone-collarbone joint in steps of a small angle to
the direction shortened the most longest spring.
B. Future Works
As foregoing developments, the repeatability of mo-
tions is the most important point for making a motion
adapted to environment the shoulder structure by learn-
ing the motions.
A simulation system should be developed with inﬂec-
tions of the force of gravity and conﬂict.
As a hardware of bladebone-collarbone structure, we
will develop new body structure which reduce conﬂict
between bladebones and the chest as little as possible,
and get more strong driving power of muscles.
