Abstract— In this paper, we propose a method to determine 
the optimal multiarticular muscle arrangement of a robot for a 
specific motion. Multiarticular muscles contribute to the reali-
zation of a musculoskeletal robot with dynamic motion ability 
similar to humans and animals. However, the ability is affected 
by the multiarticular muscle arrangement. In previous r e-
searches, the muscle arrangements of the robots were based on 
those of humans and animals. However, we suppose that a more 
suitable muscle arrangement than any other one of humans and 
animals can be found by limiting the variety of motions. 
Therefore, we focus ed on a specific motion, and furthermore, 
searched for the optimal multiarticular muscle arrangement for 
the specific motion. Specifically, we estimated the impulse 
properties of robots with a great variety of possible muscle 
arrangements. The im pulse properties were calculated using 
dynamics simula tion corresponding to the specific motion. 
Then, on the basi s of the evaluation of the impulse properties, 
we found the optimal muscle arrangement from among 736,281 
of possible muscle arrangements. Furthermore, we demon-
strated the effectiveness of our method using simulation ex-
periments. The results indicate that there is a more suitable 
muscle arrangement than any other one of humans and animals 
on condition that we focus on the specific motion.

muscles contribute to the realization of a robot with dynamic 
motion ability.

The force properties were calculated on the basis of the statics 
and kinematics of the robot [9]. We estimated a sequence of 
force properties using a sequence of robot key poses corr e-
sponding to the specific motion. On the basis of the evalu a-
tion of the sequences of the force properties, we determined 
the optimal muscle arrangement of a robot for the sp ecific 
motion. 
The previous research [10] ignored the dynamics of the 
robot and the muscle. However, in performing dynamic 
motions such as jumping and running, their dynamics cannot 
be ignored. In this research, we propose an approach in 
consideration of the dynamics of the robot and the muscle. In 
this research, we determined the optimal muscle arrangement 
by evaluating the impulse properties of a robot estimated 
using dynamics simulation corresponding to the specific 
motion (Fig. 3). However, in this research, we dealt with the 
translational impulse and did not deal with the angular i m-
pulse. 
In dynamics simulation, the generation of robot motions is 
generally a difficult problem. We generated robot motions 
using the maximum COG (the center of gravity) acceleration 
profile [10]. It is a method to calculate the combination of 
muscle tension vectors which maximizes the magnitude of 
COG acceleration of a robot in a given direction. Using the 
method, we obtained the desired muscle tension vector for 
the specific motion. 
In this research, as an example of artificial muscles, we 
dealt with PAM actuators. In the simulation, we calculated 
the muscle tension vector generated by PAM actuators 
from through the PAM actuator model, and furthermore, 
applied to the robot motion model. 
In this research, as an example of the specific motion, we 
dealt with backward jumping for back handspring (Fig. 4). 
The realization of complex and fast robot motion is important. 
It enhances the mobility of a robot. Back handspring is i n-
cluded in such interesting robot motions. 
In this research, as an example of muscle arrangement, we 
attempted to optimally arrange six PAM actuators at three 
joints in a robot leg. The three joints are ankle, knee and hip 
joint. In the case that all the muscles are monoarticular 
muscles, not less than six muscles are necessary to drive three 
joints. Therefore, we considered that the number of muscles 
was six. 
We generated a vast se arch space for the optimal muscle 
arrangement. The search space was generated from the 
number of joints and muscles, without reference to muscle 
arrangements of humans and animals [10]. We searched for 
the optimal muscle arrangement for backward jumping mo-
tion from among the search space. 
It should be noted that while we describe our solution for 
the specific example in this paper, our approach is more 
general and geared towards l arger problems of determining 
the optimal muscle arrangement for desired robot motion s. 
For example, we can easily imagine that our method can 
determine the optimal muscle arrangement of a robot for 
multiple motions by using multi -objective optimization 
methods [13].

VI. SEARCH RESULTS AND SIMULATION EXPERIMENTS 
A. Search Results 
In this paper, w e searched for the optimal muscle a r-
rangement of a robot for a backward jumping motion from 
among the above 736,281 of possible muscle arrangements . 
In this search, we supposed that was 77 [deg] using the 
knowledge of biomechanics [14]. Furthermore, we supposed 
that the maximum tension of each mus cle was the same (= 
400 [N]). We show the search result in Fig. 6. 
B. Simulation Experiments 
In order to demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, we 
performed simulation experiments. In this simulation e x-
periments, we compared the muscle arrangement found by 
our method with the muscle arrangement of a human leg [8] 
(Fig. 7). 
The number of muscles in the muscle arrangement found 
by our method (= 6) is different from that of the muscle ar-
rangement of a human leg (= 9). Therefore, we changed the 
maximum tension of each muscle of a human leg according to 
the ratio of maximum tension [8]. As a result, the total of 
maximum tension of muscle arrangement found by our 
method became the same as that of the muscle arrangement of 
a human leg (= 2,400 N). Under these conditions, the muscle 
arrangement of a human leg was evaluated for a backward 
jumping motion in the same way as we determi ned the o p-
timal muscle arrangement. We show the results in Fig. 8. 
VII. CONCLUSION 
In this paper, we focused on a specific motion, and fur-
thermore, proposed a method to determine the optimal mul-
tiarticular muscle arrangement of a robot for the specific 
motion. We determined the optimal muscle arrangement by 
evaluating impulse properties of the robot. We estimated the 
impulse properties using dynamics simulations of the robot 
and the muscle during the specific motion. We searched for 
the optimal muscle arrang ement from among 736,281 of 
possible muscle arrangements. Furthermore, we demo n-
strated the effectiveness of our method using simulation e x-
periments. As a result, the results indicate that there is a more 
suitable muscle arrangement than any other one of humans 
and animals on condition that we focus ed on the specific 
motion. In the future, using our method, we intend to realize 
dynamic motions such as back handspring by a real robot.
