Abstract – We are trying to create an innovative humanoid 
which has increased flexibility by imitating the st ructure of a 
human shoulder, especially the shoulder blade. We d esigned a 
flexible shoulder structure for a muscle-driven hum anoid robot 
which has the following merits, 1) wide range of mo vement and 
flexible shoulder like humans, 2) having the should er structure 
built on to the outside of the chest, allowing spac e for a lot of 
parts inside the chest. We developed a prototype hu manoid robot 
named “Blade” which has a musculo-skeletal humanoid torso 
with the shoulder blade, driven by motor-actuated t endons. This 
paper describes the advantages of the structure of the shoulder 
blade, the development of a humanoid with the shoul der blade, 
and some experiments showing its range of movement using the 
prototype humanoid robot we presented. 
I. I NTRODUCTION
In the future, it will be commonplace in daily life for 
humanoid robots to assist humans directly. At that time, 
humanoids must not be dangerous to humans. It will be of the 
utmost necessity that the humanoid robot has a flex ible and a 
safe body structure [1]. The flexibility of the arms will be 
especially important, because these parts will come in direct 
contact with a human body. Therefore, flexibility w ill be one 
of the keys to the next stage of humanoid research. 
From this point of view, we have developed a whole-
body tendon-driven humanoid named “Kenta” and “Kenj i” 
(Fig.1). They have a flexible spine structure, redundant DOFs 
driven by many actuators over 100. We have conducte d 
studies on flexibility of motion using the spine an d the whole 
body [2]-[6]. The shoulder design of Kenta and Kenji is a 
tendon driven structure, which uses two ball-and-so cket joints 
connected in series. It satisfies a range of moveme nt required, 
and it is flexible. Due to a lack of power in the c hest and a 
lack of space to add extra motors and boards, the h umanoid 
cannot raise its arms because of its heavy forearms. 
Most of the previous humanoid shoulder structure can 
not move with the flexibility of a human shoulder s tructure, 
because they have 3 DOFs structures which are fixed to the 
outside of the body [7]. There are few studies on shoulder 
structure, which take account into wide range of mo vement 
and flexibility[8]. However, we have never had an example of 
a humanoid, which has a flexible shoulder structure with a 
spine. The structure of the spine is also important for the 
flexibility.

II. A DVANTAGES OF HAVING SHOULDER BLADES
A. A wide range of movement 
Human shoulder structure has two joints and is a 
complex structure of scapula-humerus and scapula-ch est, 
constructed by bladebone, collerbone, breastbone an d 
humerus[9][10]. The acromioclavicular joint ( Scapula-
humerus joint) structure is a ball-and-socket joint built by the 
head of humerus and the glenoid cavity of scapula, the 
sternoclavicular joint ( scapula-chest joint) structure is a crank 
mechanism working by the function of the collerbone . From a 
top view, bladebones are rotated around the chest. The center 
of rotation is equal to the center of the body. From a back view, 
when the upper arm rotates over 70 degrees, the bla de bone 
starts rotation. Then it can be put into practice i n a wide range 
of movement that the scapula-humerus structure shif ts on the 
chest by moving the shoulder blade (Fig.2).

B. Flexibility by muscles
The shoulder structure with shoulder blade has flex ibility 
because shoulders are connected to the chest almost only by 
muscles except for collarbone-chest joints. It is l ike the 
shoulder blade is floating in muscles on the chest. When you 
slip and fall to the ground, you will protect yours elf 
automatically by using your arms. If upper arms are directly 
connected with the rib bone, the damage of falling down is 
directly carried to the chest, heart, lung and some important 
organs from arms. The flexibility of the shoulder a lso has the 
role of impact absorption. It will be also useful f or humanoid 
robotics. 
C. Location of the shoulder structure 
Humans have to protect their internal organs, espec ially 
the heart. Ribs are staffed around the heart to pro tect it, and 
shoulders are also formed on the outer surface of t he costa to 
guard from danger. In imitating this structure, we can develop 
a leading design of humanoids. The humanoid we want to 
develop has flexible spine and shoulder blades. It needs 
enough space in the chest, so the volume of the che st is 
important. Shoulders are located outside the chest, so the 
space inside the chest is large. It means we can de sign a torso 
which contains a lot of motors, boards, and sensors for strong 
shoulder, spine, and neck. 
D. The possibility of muscles to work together 
The main muscles supporting the upper torso are 
concentrated in the shoulder blades. They permit pe rformance 
of flexible movement with many muscles working in u nison. 
From the above, it is useful for Muscle-skeletal hu manoids to 
model the human shoulder blade. This structure suit s to 
tendon-driven humanoids. 
It is a good example for humanoid robots. We expect that 
humanoids will have the flexibility and wide movabl e 
shoulder range by imitating of human shoulder. It c an place 
the axis of rotation of the shoulder structure in t he center of 
human body near the spine, and place the shoulder structure 
on the outside of the chest. From that arrangement, it can 
make for a larger capacity inside the chest, and can contain the 
important parts such as motors, boards and sensors and protect 
them.

A. Skeletal structures made by art clay 
We made a prototyped humanoid to test for the flexi ble 
motion of shoulder blades. The flame constituents o f the 
prototype humanoid model, such as shoulder blade, 
collarbone, chest, head and vertebras, are made by art clay

bladebones and the head, and contains 10 DC motors and 
motor driver  boards. Many muscles connected with the 
shoulder blade are distributed over the arms, back, and neck. 
We designed the head and vertebra to seem to have a 
cooperative action when the muscles are used from the neck to 
the collarbone and bladebones just like humans. Wit h using 
natural rubber as muscles, we connect each part, and fix the 
shoulder blade to it and make it possible to move w ide range 
of motion.

B. A structure between a shoulder blade and a chest 
When a shoulder blade moves on the surface of a che st, 
these parts cause friction between the shoulder bla de and the 
costa. For example of human, muscles between a shou lder 
blade and a chest lower the friction. We generate t he effect of 
the anterior serratus muscle (a muscle between a should er 
blade and a chest) that is made of a neoprene, a material of a 
chloroprene rubber laminated surface of a jersey cl oth. The 
surface of the cloth is so smooth that it lowers th e friction 
between the rigid parts. Additionally the material has high 
elasticity, so it is expected that the cloth contri butes to the 
flexible motion of the shoulders. Then the shoulder blade can 
move up and down, adduct and abduct, and rotate on the chest 
(Fig.5).

B. Actuating a shoulder blade with muscles 
It is a challenge actuating a shoulder blade using some 
motors and steal wires, because the blade bone is n ot 
stabilized on a chest by itself. It is just like fl oating on the 
chest. The shoulder blade of human is surrounded an d 
stabilized with several muscles all over. But we ca n not 
increase the number of actuators like a human. So, we have to 
find useful layouts of muscles to control the shoul der blade. 
The motions of a shoulder blade are vertical moveme nt, 
adduction and abduction, and rotation. The layout w e choose 
must be able to perform these motions. In the proto type 
humanoid we can change the muscle layout easily, so we can 
test some layouts repeatedly to find the best way. Bladebone 
moves on the surface of the chest so we have to con sider the 
power on the surface of Fig.12. 
Fig.12 shows two kinds of diagram which present mus cle 
layouts. The left one is simplified version of huma n muscles 
involving a shoulder blade. The upper two muscles a re 
connected to the top of the neck, and the lower two muscles 
are connected to the bottom of the chest. This huma n like 
layout can perform flexible raising arm motion. Howe ver, this 
layout always needs a rigid neck structure to suppo rt shoulder 
blades. The human neck structure has a variable flexibility and 
it can control their stiffness. It must have flexib ility and 
stiffness at the same time, so it is difficult to g enerate the 
effect for a humanoid. 
The right one on Fig.12 is the layout that does not have 
muscles connected to the neck. The upper two points of the 
bladebone is actuated to the bottom, and lower two point of 
the bladebone is pulled from the topside of the che st. It also 
can perform flexible motions. When the actuator A a nd B are 
pulled by motors, the bladebone is lifted up. When the 
actuator C and D are pulled the other way by motors , it comes

C. Reaching motion based on vision sensors 
The shoulder structure with bladebone has redundant 
DOFs and it is connected to a chest of almost only muscles, so 
it is difficult to solve the kinematics of the norm al link 
structure. Consequently, one of the solutions of th is problem 
is to use vision sensors. If we want to catch something, we just 
look at it and reach our arm to it. Humans do not m ove with 
correct joint angles of each joint. Humanoid which has a body 
like a human can reach to a ball based on vision se nsors. We 
can control a length of each muscle by using feedba ck of the 
difference between the ball and the hand. Two CCD c olor 
cameras are installed in the head. Using the two ca meras, it 
can identify the difference between colors and calc ulate the 
depth by binocular stereo vision. Fig.13 shows the humanoid 
reaching to red ball motion by using vision sensors . A blue 
film is put toward a robots hand, so the robot can identify the 
position of the hand. The robot looks at the red ba ll and the 
blue hand and calculates the difference of each positions in the 
space by binocular stereo vision. The arm is actuat ed to 
reduce the difference between red ball and blue han d. Finally 
the robot can reach to the ball. 
IV. S UMMARY AND DISCUSSION
ʳ  Heading toward a next stage, we have built a flexib le 
shoulder humanoid. The human body structure suggest ed the 
novel design for muscle-skeletal humanoid with shou lder 
blades. In this paper, we proposed a step of compos ition of an 
unprecedented flexible shoulder humanoid by found t he most 
suitable position of muscles for the robot, designe d the parts 
of the shoulder blade, and developed the actuator using a cloth 
material. So it has a wide range of movement and ma kes a 
motion that is very similar to humans. Moreover, by using the 
shoulder blade, we showed that it could make space in the 
chest, and design the position of muscles to make m oments as 
useful as possible. We also introduced a way to gen erate the 
flexible motion of the shoulder blade. One difficul ty in this 
experiment was to get enough power for the shoulder. In those 
experiments, if we did not find an effective muscle layout, we 
could not achieve the desired motions. The more wei ghted the 
arm, the harder to lift it up. However we want to in stall touch 
sensors in the hands in the next step, so we should design a 
chest which can contain 20W motors actuating the arms. 
Hereafter, the next aim of our research is to devel op a 
whole body humanoid robot which has a flexible spin e and 
shoulder blades. We want to develop the parts of the chest that 
have joints at the spine, and try to achieve the mo tion which is 
created by cooperation of the arm, shoulder, and sp ine. By 
mounting touch sensors, we want to perform physical 
interaction with humans. We also want to develop a system to 
use these complex body structures with complete con trol. Our 
ultimate goal is to allow free interaction between humans and 
humanoids by perfecting the flexibility of the body.
