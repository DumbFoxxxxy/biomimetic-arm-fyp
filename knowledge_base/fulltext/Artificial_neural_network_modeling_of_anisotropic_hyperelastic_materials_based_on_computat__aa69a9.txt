 Artificial neural network modeling for 
anisotropic hyperelastic materials based on 
computational crystal structure data 
 
Sunyoung Im1, Wonbae Kim2, Hyungjun Kim3 and Maenghyo Cho4 
Multiscale Mechanical Design Division, Department of Mechanical and Aerospace 
Engineering, Seoul National University, Gwanak-ro 1, Gwanak-gu, Seoul 151-744, Republic 
of Korea 
Based on computational crystal structures da ta, a new modeling approach using 
artificial neural network for anisotropic hyperelastic materials is proposed. Compared to 
experiments, computational model for materials can simulate various behaviors of 
materials and provide information on all components of strain, stress and constitutive 
tensors. Classical hype relastic modeling methods assume the strain energy density 
function, and obtain stress and constitutive tensor by differentiating it with respect to the 
strains. However, it is not easy to find the strain energy density function that describes the 
anisotropic hyperelastic materials such as complex hexagonal and tetragonal crystal 
structures. Bypassing the assumption of the strain energy density function , this study 
directly use s the data of the st rain, stress, and constitutive tensor through machine 
learning technique . Artificial neural network ( ANN) models are constructed for face -
centered cubic (Cu), di amond cubic (Si) and hexagonal (ZnO) structures. Numerical 
simulations based on finite element method (FEM) are performed using the proposed 
ANN models in order to characterize macroscopic mechanical behaviors.

II. Introduction 
 
 Continuum mechanics consists of the compatibility for strain and displacement, the equilibrium for stress and 
force, and the constitutive law for strain and stress . The compatibility relation is based on geometry, and the 
equilibrium equation is derived from N ewton’s laws of motion. So that there are no uncertainty or error in two 
equations. I n contrast, the constitutive equation is a formulation obtained from the observations and the 
experiments, which inevitability involves the uncertainty. 
Hyperelastic mate rial is a type of constitutive model for ideal elastic material of which the stress -strain 
relationship derives from a strain energy density function. Over the decades, many hyperplastic models have been 
proposed to consider large deformation for rubberlike materials [1-5]. Those functions have material constants to 
be determined from experimental data. Classical hyperealstic model represents test data well only for certain 
levels of strains, and hardly for other conditions. Obtaining from observations and experiments, it is fundamentally 
impossible to construct a model that perfectly simulates the behavior of materials. 
 In this background, Sussman and Bathe presented a different approach which directly interpolate using spline 
function the test data ob tained, instead of fitting the material constants [6]. Since then, the model-free modeling 
approaches such as ‘What-You-Prescribe-is-What-You-Get (WYPiWYG)’ have been actively studied [7-11]. 
 Meanwhile, with these uncertainty of the constitutive relation, Ortize suggests to directly use a data set 
obtained from the physical experiments without depending on the empirical modeling [12]. This approach is 
usually called ‘data-driven’ computing which does not require any modeling. Related studies are also being carried 
out extensively [13-14]. 
 To our knowledge, however, above recent researches about ‘WYPiWYG’ or ‘data-driven approaches’ just 
focus on data processing methods with in imagined strain-stress spaces [6-14]. Despite the brilliant advances in 
computational materials [15-18], none of them uses the meaningful material data. So, it is notable that this study 
utilize the data based on com putational materials. Using computational materials rather than experimentation, it 
is realized that there is a lot of information available. Computational materials can perform the behaviors under 
various loading conditions and also give all components of strain, stress, and constitutive tensor with regard to the 
mechanical properties of the materials. 
 Based on diverse and sufficient material data, this study proposes to use artificial neural network for modeling 
the hyperelastic materials. Artificial neural network have been broadly in the various engineering applications due 
to their practicality [ 19-21]. Since ANN can identify complex nonlinear relationships between input and output 
data, it is a good alternative to classic model based on polynomial strain energy density functions. 
 For challenging tasks, this paper targets the crystal structure s as anisotropic hype rplastic material which is 
rarely reported for its complexity. With the great interest of nanoscale devices over past decades, it is meaningful 
to understand the properties of the single crystals and construct a practical model for them. The reference data of 
single crystal structure is computed using molecular mechanics (MM) and density functional theory (DFT) with 
The Cauchy -Born rule (CBR). The proposed ANN model is embedded in finite element method (FEM) and 
verified its effectiveness.

III. Computational crystal structures 
 
Some of the properties of crystalline solids depend on the crystal structure of the materials, the way atoms, 
ions, or molecules are spatially arranged. There are many different possible crystal structures, and they are divided 
into seven different crystal system according to unit cell geometry. These seven crystal systems are cubic, 
tetragonal, hexagonal, orthorhombic, rhombohedra, monoclinic, and triclinic. Among them, this study targets the 
face-centered cubic (FCC) and diamond cubic belonging to the cubic crystal structure, and wurtzite belonging to 
the hexagonal crystal structure. 
Fig. 1 shows the unit cells of (a) face-centered cubic crystal structure, (b) diamond cubic crystal structure, and 
(c) hexagonal wurtzite crystal structure.

To obtain the specific hyperelastic behavior of crystal structures, this study conducted numerical simulation 
based on molecular mechanics (MM) and density functional theory (DFT). The Cauchy-Born rule (CBR) which 
acts as the bridge between the atomistic deformation in a crystal structure and the macroscopic deformation in the 
continuum scale is also used. The macroscopic deformation gradient determine the position of each atom in the 
deformed state. Once any atomic position of a structure in a deformed configuration is defined, the stress and 
tangent modulus of the deformed state is computed analytica lly from the inter-atomic potential function. For the 
copper, the embedded-atom method (EAM) potential is used to describe metallic bonding of face -centered cubic 
(FCC) crystal. For the silicon of diamond cubic crystal structure, Tersoff potential is used to consider covalent 
boding materials. However, lacking of empirical potential precisely characterizing the wurtzite ZnO, first -
principle calculations based on DFT is used to obtain the stress -strain relationship and constitutive tensor. These 
computational materials can give all components of strain, stress, and constitutive tensor under various loading 
conditions.

IV. Classical hyperelastic modeling for crystal structures 
 
Hyperelastic is the constitutive relation for ideally elastic material where the stress-strain relationship derived 
from a strain energy density function. A strain energy density function is a function of strain, and the stress is 
obtained by differentiating it with respect to strain. For isotropic materials, the constitutive relation is the same 
regardless of the selected coordinate systems. So that a lot of the strain energy density functions have been studied 
for isotropic materials using the invariants of strain tensor such as Neo-Hookean, Mooney-Rivlin, and polynomial 
models. 
However, as these invariants are not suitable for the crystal structures which are anisotropic hyperelastic 
materials, Smith and Rivlin proposed the form-invariant quantities. The form of the strain energy density function 
is determined for each crystal systems. By limiting the order of the polynomial bases to three, an anisotropic 
hyperealstic model for crystal structures is derived as:

V. Artificial neural network modeling for crystal structures 
A. ANN modeling 
Artificial neural network (ANN), which is an algorithms simulating the functioning of the biological neurons, 
is able to be identified as data processing systems. ANN consists of numerous neurons which are also called units 
or nodes. Each node is connected to the nodes of neighboring layers, but not to nodes of same layer. The weight 
is the parameter associated with each connection. The weights correspond to the strength of connection between 
the interconnected nodes. Training is the process to find the optimal value of weights. 
The advantage of ANN is that it does not require any mathematical model, it just learns from the exa mples 
and recognizes patterns in a series of input and output data. With sufficient data about all components of strain, 
stress, and constitutive tensor in various loading conditions , ANN model can be an efficient alternati ve of 
complicated hyperelastic model of crystal structures.

B. Application of ANN model to crystal structures 
This study applied the proposed ANN model for copper, silicon and zinc-oxide. These models are established 
and trained within the Matlab programming environment. The trained data were constructed on a total of 19 
different loading conditions: x-, y-, and z-direction for the uniaxial deformation; xy-, yz-, and zx-direction for the 
pure shear and si mple shear deformation; x,y-, y,z - , and z,x -direction for the biaxial deformation; triaxial 
deformation of strain ratio (1:1:1), (1:-0.5:-0.5), and ( 1:1:-1). The strain range of copper is from -15% to 15%, 
and the strain range of silicon and zinc -oxide is from -14% to 14%. Both ranges are much wider than the range 
used in classical Kim model. 
Table 1 shows the mate rial constants of 17 parameter models for copper and silicon . Since the material 
constants are based on the data of 19 different loading condition with wide ranges, their values are slightly 
different from those in the literature. 
For copper, Fig. 3 shows the second Piola-Kirchhoff (2nd PK) stress of reference data, Kim model, and ANN 
model under four basic deformation conditions: (a) uniaxial, (b) biaxial, (c) pure shear, and (d) triaxial behaviors. 
Kim model matches the reference data within a narrow str ain range, but the larger the range, the larger the 
difference between them. On the other hand, the ANN model tends to match the refere nce data over the entire 
strain range. Fig. 4 represents the tangent modulus of copper in basic loading conditions. And it shows a clearer 
performance difference between the two models. For the trained data, it is easily confirmed that the ANN model 
follows the reference data more accurately than Kim model. The validation results for strain ratio ( 1:0.5:0) is 
shown in Fig. 5 . The ANN model predicts both 2 nd PK stress and tangent modulus more accurately than Kim 
model. 
The ANN model of silicon also shown the same trend as shown in Fig. 6, 7, 8 in which the ANN model more 
accurately follows the reference data than Kim model. In the case of zinc oxide, there is no existing comparable 
model, so ANN model is only compared with the reference data as shown in Fig. 9, 10, 11. What is notable is that 
the biaxial behavior of Fig. 9 (b) and Fig. 10 (b) is somewhat complicated, but the ANN model delicately describes 
it.

VI. Numerical example 
A. Tension test of a notched bar 
In order to investigate the applicability of the proposed ANN model, nonlinear finite element analysis of the 
notched specimen 12 is performed. The notched specimen is under a uniform tensile loading as shown in Fig. 12. 
The specimen has a dimension of 120 n m in length, 25 nm in width, and 10 nm in thickness. The notched part 
located at the center of the specimen has a dimension of 40 nm in length, and 15 nm in width. The specimen is 
modeled using 480 8 -node hexahedral elements with 796 nodes. The single crys tal copper is adopted as a test 
material. One edge marked A in Fig. 12 is fixed in the x -direction, and the other end marked B is subjected a 
tensile loading uniformly with a force of 250 nN.

VII. Conclusion 
This study suggested to use artificial neural network (ANN) models for the anisotropic hyperelasticity based 
on data of computational materials. Data on material behaviors of copper, silicon, and zinc -oxide was obtained 
from molecular mechanics (MM) and density functional theory (DFT) in various loading conditions . Without 
assuming the strain ener gy density function, two types of ANN model were constructed just based on sufficient 
data. One is a model with six strain components as inputs and six stress components as outputs. The other is a 
model with six strain components as inputs and 21 constitut ive tensor components as outputs. It was confirmed 
that ANN models not only simulate accurately simulate the trained loading condition, but also can predict the 
untrained loading condition compared to classical hyperelastic model . ANN model is simply embedded into a 
nonlinear finite element analysis framework. To validate its applicability, the notched bar of copper was analyzed 
under uniform tensile load. Since the numerical results of ANN FEM model was almost identical to those of the 
CBR FEM model, its accuracy is verified.
